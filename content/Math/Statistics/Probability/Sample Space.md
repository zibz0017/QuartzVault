# Definition
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A listing of all of the different possible outcomes in a certain probabilistic event.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Note that this does not tell you how likely each event is. It could be that every event has the same probability as any other, but it could be not.</span>
## Compound Events
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This is where there are multiple sub-events which are part of a larger event.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In such a situation, the sample space is all of the possibilities of the sub-events matches together.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A useful way to think of all of the events can be like a tree-diagram, or like a table (see [[#Coin Flip and Die Roll]]).</span>
# Examples
## Coin Flip
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The sample space of a coin flip is heads or tails. It does not matter if the coin is a fair coin or not (meaning, whether it is equally likely to land on heads or tails, or it is not), the sample space remains the same.</span>
## Die Roll
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The sample space of a standard six-sided die is 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6.</span>
## Coin Flip and Die Roll
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The sample space of a coin flip and a die roll (meaning, a person is doing both a coin flip and a die roll) is every different possibility for the coin flip matched with every possibility of the die roll.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As such, the sample space will be 1 heads, 2 heads, 3 heads, 4, heads, 5 heads, 6 heads, 1 tails, 2 tails, 3 tails, 4 tails, 5 tails, and 6 tails.</span>
