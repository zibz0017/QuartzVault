# Definition
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A measure of how well a straight line fits the data. This measures how much the change in $y$ is correlated (like how "correlated" is normally used, not like the math concept of [[Correlation|correlation]]) with the change in $x$.</span>
# Specific Points
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This will always be between 0 and 1.</span> ^53970f
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The closer to 1, the greater the change in $y$ is related to the change in $x$. The closer to $0$, the less it is related.</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">See the [[Correlation#Specific Points|specific points discussed by correlation]] as they apply to the coefficient of determination, as well.</span>
# Formula
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The symbol for coefficient of determination is $r^2$.</span>
## Version 1 of the Formula
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As implied from the symbol, the coefficient of determination equals the [[Correlation|correlation]] ($r$) squared.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As [[#^53970f|mentioned earlier]] the coefficient of determination will always be between 0 and 1. This makes sense since $r$ will always be between -1 and 1. Any value between -1 and 1 squared will equal between 0 and 1.</span>

## Version 2 of the Formula
$$1-\frac{sum \space of \space squared \space residuals \space from \space regression \space line}{sum \space of \space squared \space residuals \space from \space \overline{y}}$$
## Intuition
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">$\overline{y}$ (the average of just the $y$ values) is the prediction one would make for a theoretical next value of $y$, if there were no $x$ value. This is the same thing one would do whenever they have a set of data and they have to make a prediction for the future from that data. For example, if you average an 80 on your tests, it would be reasonable to guess that you will get around an 80 on your nest test (putting aside the fact that maybe you'll feel more motivated to do better and all those kinds of things).</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">However, the $x$ value may be able to help us predict the $y$ value. For example, if you are measuring size and weight, if you knew the person's size, that should affect your guess for their weight.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The $sum \space of \space squared \space residuals \space from \space \overline{y}$ in the fraction represents the error for predicting the $y$ values without using the $x$ values at all. The $sum \space of \space squared \space residuals \space from \space regression \space line$ in the fraction represents the error for predicting the $y$ values using the $x$ values.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As such, $\frac{sum \space of \space squared \space residuals \space from \space regression \space line}{sum \space of \space squared \space residuals \space from \space \overline{y}}$ represents what percentage of $y$ value is not explained by the $x$ value. For example, if the $sum \space of \space squared \space residuals \space from \space regression \space line$ is relatively low, and the $sum \space of \space squared \space residuals \space from \space \overline{y}$ is relatively high, that means that the regression line does a very good job of predicting the $y$ values using the $x$ values, meaning that the $y$ values are very related to the $x$ values.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">To add a bit of explanation, the residual is the amount that $y$ has been incorrectly predicted. The fraction of $\frac{sum \space of \space squared \space residuals \space from \space regression \space line}{sum \space of \space squared \space residuals \space from \space \overline{y}}$ is the ratio between how inaccurately $y$ has been predicted based off the regression line, and just using the average value of $y$. The average value of $y$ is the best estimate that we can make for $y$ without using $x$, at all. As such, the fraction represents the ratio between the inaccuracy in predicting $y$ using our regression line and making our best guess without using $x$ at all.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Now, to find the opposite, meaning, how much the $y$ value *is* related to the $x$ value, we need to subtract the fraction from 1 (like if 3/4 of a pizza was eaten and you want to find out how much is left, you need to subtract 3/4 from 1 which equals 1/4 ($1-3/4=1/4$ )). And that is the formula.</span>
## Coefficient of Determination vs Correlation
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As mentioned, the coefficient of determination measures how accurate the regression line is compared to simply using $\overline{y}$. The number is intuitive, meaning, we can understand what it is we are measuring. In addition, we can compare 2 different coefficients of determination, meaning, the if the coefficient of determination for one set of data with a certain regression line is half that of a different set of data, we know that the $x$ value helps us predict the $y$ value in the second set of data, twice as well as it does in the first.</span>
