## דברי אמת
תנא דבי אליהו tells us that Hashem desires people to do תשובה more than a parent desires his child or a wife desires her husband.
# Shmuess 2
## ישעיה נה ח-ט רש"י שם
Hashem is not like people. When it comes to people, when they admit they are wrong (in בית דין), they are חייב to pay restitution. This is not so when someone admits they did wrong to Hashem. If someone admits he his wrongdoing and abandons his ways, Hashem will have רחמנות on him.

Furthermore, Hashem wants of people is greater than what people want for themselves. Their plans are lead to lowness and Hashem's plans lead to greatness.
## Shmuess
### Point 1
 The פסוק is clearly supposed to be giving people inspiration to do תשובה. The ראש ישיבה asked why is there a point of focusing on the *contrast* between people and Hashem? The פסוק should simply focus in the fact that Hashem will have רחמנות on people who do abandon their ways.
 The reason for the contrast seems to be that it further drives home the point of Hashem's kindness for us.
 We see how much of a powerful impetus focusing on Hashem's kindness toward us is in terms of getting us to try and do תשובה.
### Point 2
The פסוק talks about how what Hashem wants for people is leads to greatness and what a person wants leads to lowliness. It seems that this message is being given to everyone, even (and, perhaps, especially) רשעים.
We see that even רשעים are able to relate to these lofty ideals about how following Hashem leads to true greatness.
# Shmuess 3
## דברים כז ז ספורנו שם
כלל ישראל was bringing קרבנות and rejoicing over the coming pronouncements at הר גריזים and הר עיבל.
## Shmuess
It makes sense that כלל ישראל was rejoicing over the pronouncements at הר עיבל as those were ברכות to כלל ישראל for following the words of Hashem, but how does it make sense that they rejoiced over the words at הר גריזים?
It seems that if one has the proper perspective on Hashem's punishments, they will rejoice from the thought of punishment for doing the wrong thing. Punishment is something that keeps us in line and prevents us from doing the wrong thing. If one has this perspective, they can not only avoid thinking about punishment as a negative, but can even think of it as a positive. (See the following shmuess)
