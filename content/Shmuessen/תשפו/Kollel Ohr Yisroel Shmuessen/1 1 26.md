# בראשית מח ב רש"י שם
1. יעקב sat up in his bed when יוסף came in with Menashe and אפרים. He did this to give kavod to יוסף because he was a king.
2. We learn from here that one should give kavod to a king.
3. Similarly, משה gave kavod to Pharoah, and Eliyahu gave kavod to a king <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">need to find out which</span> as well.
# Shmuess
1. It appears that according to רש"י, יעקב only afforded יוסף this kavod because he was a king. Not because יוסף was a צדיק, or because he was the progenitor of יעקב legacy, or because he loved, or any other reason.
2. The first thing we see is that we see how calculated יעקב was. Maybe יעקב did this for any of the other reasons mentioned. The fact that רש"י can learn something from this shows us that יעקב was being calculated and so we can learn this from here.
3. Second, many times parents have a hard time seeing their kids as anything other than their little children, no matter how far they have gone in life. Clearly, יעקב saw right through this and appreciated יוסף for what he did.
4. Thirdly, we see an angle into דרך ארץ. How can we learn from what יעקב did for יוסף to משה giving kavod to Pharoah or Eliyahu giving kavod to <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">whoever it was. He was a Rasha</span>. We see that kavod liמלכות is a לא פלוג to all kings. Simply the fact that the person is a king makes it that they are worthy of kavod, regardless of how much of a rasha they are.
   This would seemingly apply to giving kavod to any tzelem Elokim. Even though they may be tremendous rishaim, that doesn't take away from the kavod they deserve for being a tzelem Elokim.
