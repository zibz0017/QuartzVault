# ויקרא טז א-ב רש"י ד"ה וידבר ה' (לפי משכיל לדוד, ספר זכרון)
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">the תורה tells us that Hashem spoke to משה after Nadav and Avihu died. Hashem then told משה to tell Aharon about when one shouldn't come to the משכן.
Why is the תורה telling us when Hashem was speaking to משה?
The תורה is teaching us that Hashem told משה to use the example of the fact that Aharon's two sons died, meaning, that משה to tell Aharon "Don't come in to the משכן at the wrong time so that you don't die like your sons".
</span>
# Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Why is it necessary for משה to specifically mention the point that he will end up like Aharon's sons if he isn't careful. Aharon just lost his sons. Simply telling him not to go into the משכן at the wrong time should be enough. It is obvious, given the context, why this is being said now. There is no need to specifically mention the fact that his sons just died.
Additionally, it is painful for Aharon to be reminded of his sons dying. Even though it would be clear that this הלכה is being given because of the deaths of Nadav and Avihu, which is certainly a reminder to Aharon, specifically mentioning it is certainly more painful.
Additionally, we are dealing with Aharon. He obviously wants to do the ratzon Hashem. This doesn't seem remotely necessary.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see an that every little bit of making something more real and more tangible can make a difference, even for someone like Aharon, and it is even worth perhaps causing him a little pain to help him follow this הלכה with the greatest degree of feeling.</span>
