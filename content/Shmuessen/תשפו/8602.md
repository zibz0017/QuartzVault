# Shmuess 1
## בראשית כז טו
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">When Yaakov was going to get the brachos from Yitzchak, Rivka gave him Eisav's garments.</span>
## רש"י ד"ה החמדת
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">These were the garments that were originally worn by Nimrod, which Eisav took from him.</span>
## דעת זקנים על רש"י
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">These garments had extremely vivid pictures of every animal on them and so animals would come to someone who was wearing these garments and would actually walk into traps. Rivka gave them to Yaakov so that Yaakov would feel more like Eisav.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see the power that some external action has on a person's actions. We are dealing with a monumental moment in terms of the fate of Klal Yisroel, and Yaakov personally. If Yaakov doesn't successfully trick Yitzchak, Yaakov would receive a terrible curse. In addition, Klal Yisroel would not receive the brachos. It would certainly seem that Yaakov is going to do his utmost to be successful.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see that this is not the case. It seems that even in such a situation, having the garments of Eisav can make an impact and get Yaakov to act more like Eisav.</span>
# Shmuess 2
## סנהדרין צב: רש"י ד"ה אל ישנה
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">When חנניה, מישאל, and עזריה were being thrown into the fire, they made sure to wear respectful clothing.</span>
## מהרש"א ד"ה לא ישנה
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This was because it isn't proper for a person to make themselves appear like they are pained by what Hashem has decreed for them. Instead, they should accept it with love and joy.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Once again, we see the power that clothing can have on a person. We are talking about great tzaddikim who are about to do the greatest mitzva one can do (dying for Hashem). Of course they are going to do their utmost to put themselves in the proper mindset for this mitzva.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Apparently, even so, the clothing that they wear can make a difference.</span>
# Shmuess 3
## בראשית רבא נה ח (כב ב)
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Love and hatred can be "מקלקלת את השורה" ("ruin good judgement". While this sounds negative, it clearly depends, as is clear from the medrash).</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see how love can do this because when Yaakov was coming down to Mitzraim to see Yosef, we see that Yosef went and saddled his own horse, himself, despite the fact that he had many servants that were able to do this. This was because of his tremendous love of his father.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Similarly, we see that when Klal Yisroel was by the Yam Suf, Pharoah saddled his own horse to go and chase after them, despite the fact that he, too, had many servants who could have done this for him. This was because of his tremendous hatred of Klal Yisroel.
When Klal Yisroel was by the Yam, the zechus of Yosef's saddling his own horse "stood up against" Pharoah's saddling and helped Klal Yisroel escape.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This is an incredibly pivotal moment in history. Klal Yisroel is by the Yam Suf. If they don't get away, they will all be killed and that would be the end. This zechus of Yosef is what comes in and saves them.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This is not even Yosef's greatest mitzva. Yosef's greatest mitzva would seem to be how he ran away from Eishes Potifar. This is why he is called Yosef Hatzaddik. This mitzva, while it certainly was a tremendous honor to Yaakov, would hardly seem difficult. Yosef loved his father and this was the first time he was going to see him in 22 years.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is mashma from the medrash that there is something unique about this zechus that made it able to help out at this time particularly.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It seems that there are times that for whatever cheshbonos Hashem has, our mitzvos can have monumentally disproportionate impacts.</span>
# Shmuess 4
*I don't know the background to this story, but I don't think that it is relevant*
## ירמיה לח יג
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">ירמיהו was saved from a pit with some ropes and rags.</span>
## ילקוט שמעוני על פרק לח (רמז שכו)
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">ירמיהו asked Hashem why he was saved with ropes and rags rather than a ladder. Hashem responded that this was because his great ancestor, Rachav, let the spies down by a rope, rather than a ladder.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rachav's action was tremendously great. She betrayed her own people and helped strangers, putting herself at risk. Because she did this, she was zoche to have numerous neviim descend from her (including ירמיהו).</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">However, within her action there was a slight lack, and because of this lack, this came out with her descendant, hundreds of years later.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see how nothing is overlooked or forgotten. And if this is done for negative, than this is certainly done for the positive.
# Own Thoughts</span>
## On Shmuess 1
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">I don't fully understand the דעת זקנים. They seem to be wondering why it was important that Yaakov wore these garments that had these tremendous qualities to them. I don't see how this was answered. Why couldn't Rivka have given Yaakov any other of Eisav's garments? Why did she give him these particularly?</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Perhaps the fact that Eisav wore these when he went hunting made it that these would have a greater impact on Yaakov? I'm not sure.</span>
## On Shmuess 2
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The Maharasha does not appear to clearly say why חנניה, מישאל, and עזריה should wear their nicest clothing. It could have nothing to do with having the proper feelings. Perhaps it is because a person who is doing such a mitzva should dress honorably, the same way a person dresses honorably for shabbos or similar mitzvos. A person doesn't dress honorably for shabbos to try and influence one's internal feelings. A person does this because shabbos is something that should be honored. Even if dressing nicely didn't affect one's feelings, we would still dress nicely for shabbos.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yosef Porush had the same question and asked it to Rabbi Harris. Rebbi told him that it was mashma that it actually affected their emotions from the second half of the Maharasha ("Instead, they should accept it with love and joy.") It doesn't say that they should *appear* to accept it this way. It says that they should actually accept it this way. This would seem to indicate that their feelings changed because of the way that they dressed.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">I am unsure how to read the first half of the Maharasha ("This was because it isn't proper for a person to make themselves appear like they are pained by what Hashem has decreed for them." Appearance is mentioned here.) Perhaps the way to read it is that the point is really about appearances. Looking pained about Hashem's decree (especially for great tzaddikim) can really ruin the kiddush Hashem. However, the way that the tzaddikim feel will be clear, and it will telegraph their feelings far more than their clothing will.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As such, the whole point of everything is really to give off an impression of acceptance of Hashem's plan. The main potential impediment to this is the way that חנניה, מישאל, and עזריה feel. If they are not fully onboard, this will be clear. As such, they wear these nice clothing to help put them in the right state of mind.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">I am not sure about all of this because it still seems a bit strange to me that the clothing that they are wearing won't make it extremely clear how they are feeling. As such, why is the focus of the Maharasha about wearing the clothing for the sake of changing how they feel (especially considering that it is hard to say that this would make a drastic difference, as that would be too much of a chiddush) more so, than simply the message given off by the clothing themselves?</span>
## On Shmuess 3
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At first glance it would seem that Pharoah's diligence in his hatred of Klal Yisroel almost acted as a "zechus" to help him catch them (for which Yosef's zechus had to counter it). This sounds interesting to me that Hashem will be more likely to make a negative action done with tremendous drive more likely to work, simply because it was done with that drive. I don't really understand why this would be.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">However, this may not be the case. Perhaps Klal Yisroel needed a zechus, regardless, and really Pharoah's action didn't add anything to the likelihood of Klal Yisroel's capture. It was almost excuse to allow Yosef's action to "plug in" and help save Klal Yisroel. Yosef's action was a positive action done with tremendous care. As such, it could only come to the aid of Klal Yisroel (in this disproportionate way) only if they were fighting an action which was also done with such alacrity. But this could be true whether the alacrity of Pharoah actually made it more likely for him to be victorious or not.</span>
