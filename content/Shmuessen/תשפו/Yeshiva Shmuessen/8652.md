# Shmuess 2
## &lrm;בראשית מב ז רד"ק
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The brothers did not recognize יוסף because he grew a beard, but also because they had sold him as a slave and so they were not expecting to see יוסף as the king. Even though this person did somewhat resemble יוסף (as it was actually him), it is not so uncommon for a person to resemble someone else.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The brothers knew that יוסף had a dream about him being king. And, obviously, they knew that Hashem could do anything. 
In addition, when יוסף starts causing problems for the brothers, they realize that this may be because they sold יוסף. They clearly are realizing that they made a mistake about how they judged יוסף. It would seem reasonable that perhaps they had made a mistake in how they dismissed his dreams as well.
In addition, later, יוסף seats all of the brothers by age order, which, naturally, would have been nearly impossible as all of the brothers were born within a few years of each other.
In addition, even when יוסף admits that he is יוסף, the brothers are so shocked that they don't actually believe that it is him. יוסף then shows them his ברית מילה and they still don't believe him. He finally starts talking to them in לשון הקדש and then they finally believe him.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see how when someone has a certain assumption, it takes evidence beyond belief to uproot that assumption. This is even for great people.</span>
# Shmuess 3
## &lrm;במדבר רבה ד י (ג מז), פירוש מהרז"ו שם
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There were 22,000 לויים that needed to be made קדוש and 22,273 בכורים that needed to lose their קדושה. As such, 273 were going to have to have their קדושה redeemed onto 5 סלעים (which they would have to pay).
To decide who would have to pay, they did a גורל. There is a מחלוקת about how the גורל was conducted.
The first opinion holds that they had 22,000 lots that said "לוי" on them and 273 lots with "5 סלעים" on them.
The second opinion holds that there were 22,273 lots with "לוי" written on them and 273 lots with "5 סלעים" on them.
The reason for this second opinion was because perhaps there can be a situation where all of the "לוי" lots are picked and there are still many people left (the מהרז"ו implies that perhaps all, or nearly all, of the "5 סלעים" lots are left) and people will have טענות that if they had a turn to pick, they would have been זוכה to pick a "לוי" lot. To dispel this, there were enough lots to make sure that everyone had a shot at getting a lot that said "לוי". (Hashem made a נס and all of the lots that said "5 סלעים" ended up being chosen at some point.)</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This complaint doesn't seem logical.
First of all, if Hashem arranged the situation to be that by the time it got to your turn there were only lots that said "5 סלעים" left, clearly Hashem wanted you to pay 5 סלעים. You זכותים clearly were not going to help.
Second of all, this is totally against probability. In terms of probability, it does not matter where in the order you pick. The odds are the same.
Third of all, the מהרז"ו seems to imply that this is only a concern if there are large quantities of people (he actually says that all of the lots that said "לוי" are picked first, leaving all of the lots that say "5 סלעים" left) that are stuck with only "5 סלעים" lots. If there are only a small amount of people left then this is not a concern. But shouldn't the fact that this happens indicate to the people that this is divinely ordained? The odds of this happening by chance are astronomically low. Clearly Hashem is orchestrating the results of this גורל.
Despite all of this, apparently this person will still think these things and it is so embedded and ingrained that the structure of the raffle had to rely on a נס to be successful to account for such a concern.</span>
## Side note
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rebbi was wondering why they had to set up the raffle this way (which involved relying on a נס) rather than setting up the raffle like normal and having בטחון that Hashem wouldn't make the results extremely skewed.
Rebbi thought that perhaps, משה and אהרן simply had to do their job. Their job was to make a raffle that the people would accept and so they did that in the best way that they were able to do so, despite the fact that they had to rely on a נס. They couldn't set up a raffle that could lead to טענות.</span>
# Shmuess 4
## במדבר יד מ רבינו בחיי
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">After the בני ישראל sinned with the מרגלים and were told that they were not allowed to enter ארץ ישראל, some people decided to go and fight with the כנענים anyway, against Hashem's will.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Throughout much of their time in the מדבר, whenever something would happen, people in כלל ישראל would complain and say that they wanted to appoint a new leader and head back to מצרים. Certainly now, when they were told that they would all die in the מדבר, this would have been an opportune time to want to go back to מצרים. Why didn't anyone say that they should do that now?
The reason the people wanted to go back to מצרים in general started when they saw all of the mitzriim dead by the ים סוף. They realized that instead of fighting against the 7 nations, they would be able to easily conquer מצרים as their whole army got wiped out. Additionally, it was a place that they were used to.
These people were constantly hoping that Hashem would agree with them and be willing to reverse course and go back to מצרים. In fact, this was why they sent spies: so that they would see that כנען is insurmountable and Hashem would therefore allow them to return to מצרים and live there.
(In fact, this desire to return to מצרים was not born of a rebellious nature. They were hoping that Hashem would agree with them.)
