# Shmuess 1
## &lrm;בראשית כז טו
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">When יעקב was going to get the ברכות from יצחק, רבקא gave him עשו's garments.</span>
## &lrm;רש"י ד"ה החמדת
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">These were the garments that were originally worn by Nimrod, which עשו took from him.</span>
## &lrm;דעת זקנים על רש"י
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">These garments had extremely vivid pictures of every animal on them and so animals would come to someone who was wearing these garments and would actually walk into traps. רבקא gave them to יעקב so that יעקב would feel more like עשו.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see the power that some external action has on a person's actions. We are dealing with a monumental moment in terms of the fate of כלל ישראל, and יעקב personally. If יעקב doesn't successfully trick יצחק, יעקב would receive a terrible curse. In addition, כלל ישראל would not receive the ברכות. It would certainly seem that יעקב is going to do his utmost to be successful.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see that this is not the case. It seems that even in such a situation, having the garments of עשו can make an impact and get יעקב to act more like עשו.</span>
# Shmuess 2
## &lrm;סנהדרין צב: רש"י ד"ה אל ישנה
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">When חנניה, מישאל, and עזריה were being thrown into the fire, they made sure to wear respectful clothing.</span>
## &lrm;מהרש"א ד"ה לא ישנה
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This was because it isn't proper for a person to make themselves appear like they are pained by what Hashem has decreed for them. Instead, they should accept it with love and joy.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Once again, we see the power that clothing can have on a person. We are talking about great צדיקים who are about to do the greatest מצוה one can do (dying for Hashem). Of course they are going to do their utmost to put themselves in the proper mindset for this מצוה.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Apparently, even so, the clothing that they wear can make a difference.</span>
# Shmuess 3
## &lrm;בראשית רבא נה ח (כב ב)
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Love and hatred can be "מקלקלת את השורה" ("ruin good judgement". While this sounds negative, it clearly depends, as is clear from the מדרש).</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see how love can do this because when יעקב was coming down to מצרים to see יוסף, we see that יוסף went and saddled his own horse, himself, despite the fact that he had many servants that were able to do this. This was because of his tremendous love of his father.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Similarly, we see that when כלל ישראל was by the Yam Suf, פרעה saddled his own horse to go and chase after them, despite the fact that he, too, had many servants who could have done this for him. This was because of his tremendous hatred of כלל ישראל.
When כלל ישראל was by the Yam, the זכות of יוסף's saddling his own horse "stood up against" פרעה's saddling and helped כלל ישראל escape.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This is an incredibly pivotal moment in history. כלל ישראל is by the Yam Suf. If they don't get away, they will all be killed and that would be the end. This זכות of יוסף is what comes in and saves them.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This is not even יוסף's greatest מצוה. יוסף's greatest מצוה would seem to be how he ran away from אישת פוטיפר. This is why he is called יוסף Hatzaddik. This מצוה, while it certainly was a tremendous honor to יעקב, would hardly seem difficult. יוסף loved his father and this was the first time he was going to see him in 22 years.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is משמע from the מדרש that there is something unique about this זכות that made it able to help out at this time particularly.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It seems that there are times that for whatever cheshbonos Hashem has, our מצוות can have monumentally disproportionate impacts.</span>
# Shmuess 4
*I don't know the background to this story, but I don't think that it is relevant*
## &lrm;ירמיה לח יג
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">ירמיהו was saved from a pit with some ropes and rags.</span>
## &lrm;ילקוט שמעוני על פרק לח (רמז שכו)
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">ירמיהו asked Hashem why he was saved with ropes and rags rather than a ladder. Hashem responded that this was because his great ancestor, רחב, let the spies down by a rope, rather than a ladder.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">רחב's action was tremendously great. She betrayed her own people and helped strangers, putting herself at risk. Because she did this, she was זוכה to have numerous נביאים descend from her (including ירמיהו).</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">However, within her action there was a slight lack, and because of this lack, this came out with her descendant, hundreds of years later.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see how nothing is overlooked or forgotten. And if this is done for negative, than this is certainly done for the positive.
# Own Thoughts</span>
## On Shmuess 1
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">I don't fully understand the דעת זקנים. They seem to be wondering why it was important that יעקב wore these garments that had these tremendous qualities to them. I don't see how this was answered. Why couldn't רבקא have given יעקב any other of עשו's garments? Why did she give him these particularly?</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Perhaps the fact that עשו wore these when he went hunting made it that these would have a greater impact on יעקב? I'm not sure.</span>
## On Shmuess 2
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The מהרש"א does not appear to clearly say why חנניה, מישאל, and עזריה should wear their nicest clothing. It could have nothing to do with having the proper feelings. Perhaps it is because a person who is doing such a מצוה should dress honorably, the same way a person dresses honorably for שבת or similar מצוות. A person doesn't dress honorably for שבת to try and influence one's internal feelings. A person does this because שבת is something that should be honored. Even if dressing nicely didn't affect one's feelings, we would still dress nicely for שבת.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yosef Porush had the same question and asked it to Rabbi Harris. Rebbi told him that it was משמע that it actually affected their emotions from the second half of the מהרש"א ("Instead, they should accept it with love and joy.") It doesn't say that they should *appear* to accept it this way. It says that they should actually accept it this way. This would seem to indicate that their feelings changed because of the way that they dressed.</span> 
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">I am unsure how to read the first half of the מהרש"א ("This was because it isn't proper for a person to make themselves appear like they are pained by what Hashem has decreed for them." Appearance is mentioned here.) Perhaps the way to read it is that the point is really about appearances. Looking pained about Hashem's decree (especially for great צדיקים) can really ruin the kiddush Hashem. However, the way that the צדיקים feel will be clear, and it will telegraph their feelings far more than their clothing will.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As such, the whole point of everything is really to give off an impression of acceptance of Hashem's plan. The main potential impediment to this is the way that חנניה, מישאל, and עזריה feel. If they are not fully onboard, this will be clear. As such, they wear these nice clothing to help put them in the right state of mind.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">I am not sure about all of this because it still seems a bit strange to me that the clothing that they are wearing won't make it extremely clear how they are feeling. As such, why is the focus of the מהרש"א about wearing the clothing for the sake of changing how they feel (especially considering that it is hard to say that this would make a drastic difference, as that would be too much of a חידוש) more so, than simply the message given off by the clothing themselves?</span>
## On Shmuess 3
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At first glance it would seem that פרעה's diligence in his hatred of כלל ישראל almost acted as a "זכות" to help him catch them (for which יוסף's זכות had to counter it). This sounds interesting to me that Hashem will be more likely to make a negative action done with tremendous drive more likely to work, simply because it was done with that drive. I don't really understand why this would be.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">However, this may not be the case. Perhaps כלל ישראל needed a זכות, regardless, and really פרעה's action didn't add anything to the likelihood of כלל ישראל's capture. It was almost excuse to allow יוסף's action to "plug in" and help save כלל ישראל. יוסף's action was a positive action done with tremendous care. As such, it could only come to the aid of כלל ישראל (in this disproportionate way) only if they were fighting an action which was also done with such alacrity. But this could be true whether the alacrity of פרעה actually made it more likely for him to be victorious or not.</span>
