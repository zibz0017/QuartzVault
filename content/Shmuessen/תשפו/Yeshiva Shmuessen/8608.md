# Shmuess 1
## מכות יא:
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One has to do hataras nedarim on a nidui. We see this from Yehuda. Yehuda guaranteed to Yaakov that he would make sure to bring Binyomin safely back from מצרים, and if he did not, he would forfeit his share of עולם הבא.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Even though Yehuda did successfully do this, he never was mattir neder. Because of this, he was not allowed into עולם הבא for many years.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Eventually, משה davened on his behalf. He asked Hashem to let Yehuda into עולם הבא because he helped Reuven do תשובה for his aveirah (with moving the Yaakov's bed). Reuven was inspired to admit to his aveirah after he saw Yehuda publicly admit to his aveirah with Tamar.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">משה's tefilos were successful and Yehuda was allowed to enter עולם הבא</span>
## תוס' ד"ה מי גרם שם
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The Medrash (see below) says that by mechiras יוסף (which was before Yehuda's aveirah with Tamar), Reuven wasn't there because he was at home, fasting over his aveirah with Yaakov's bed.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It seems that Reuven began the תשובה process, but he hadn't publicly admitted to his aveirah. After taking inspiration from Yehuda's action, Reuven also publicly admitted to his aveirah.</span>
## בראשית רבה פד יט (לז כט)
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">During mechiras יוסף, Reuven was at home, fasting and mourning over his aveirah with Yaakov's bed.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Reuven was the first person in history to do תשובה with such sincerity.[^1] Because of this, Hashem rewarded Reuven with having Hoshea descend from him who would help כלל ישראל do תשובה.</span>
## סוטה י:
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Because יוסף was מקדש שם שמים in private (referring to the the story with אשת פוטיפר), Hashem rewarded him by adding a letter of Hashem's name to יוסף's name. There is a passuk which refers to יוסף as יהוסף, which is יוסף with an extra "ה".</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Because Yehuda was מקדש שם שמים in public  (referring to the Yehuda admitting by the story with Tamar), Hashem rewarded him by having his entire name filled with letters of Hashem's name (the name יהודה has the entire name of Hashem in it. It seems that when Leah was naming Yehuda, Hashem inspired her to give this name to Yehuda, because Hashem knew he would do this tremendous act later in his life). In addition, because Yehuda saved the life of Tamar and the two unborn children, Hashem saved the life of 3 of Yehuda's descendants, חנניה, מישאל, and עזריה.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">משה was trying to help Yehuda get into עולם הבא. The zechus that משה invokes is not the simply the act of Yehuda admitting to his aveirah with Tamar. משה invokes the positive impact that it had on Reuven.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The act of admitting to the aveirah with Tamar was a powerful zechus. We see from the 'גמ in סוטה, the zechus from this was, in a way, more powerful than יוסף's act of refraining from אשת פוטיפר.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Not only that, but Reuven already did a high degree of תשובה on his own without inspiration from Yehuda. We see from בראשית רבה that Reuven was already doing תשובה on his own, and this act of תשובה was so sincere that it was regarded as being the first act of תשובה the history, and Reuven received the tremendous reward of having a descendant like Hoshea.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yehuda's act certainly inspired Reuven to do more, but he had already done a tremendous amount on his own.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Despite all this, the fact that it helped Reuven in whatever way it did is the zechus that משה invoked to get Yehuda into עולם הבא.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see from here the tremendous power of helping others with their spiritual growth.</span>
## Applications
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rebbi pointed out that if the bnei hayeshiva would be mechaeik themselves in a variety of ways, they would perhaps be able to positively impact the atmosphere of the yeshiva and help others grow. This would be a tremendous zechus to the person. Rebbi gave some examples (although he explicitly said that one should not be medayeik from what he did say vs what he didn't say. These were just some things that he happened to think of) such as coming on time to seder, not bateling by seder, not taking a coffee break immediately after mussar seder, and trying to maintain a positive attitude about yeshiva, even in the face of real (or perceived) grievances against the yeshiva.</span>
# Shmuess 2
## בראשית מט לא רמב"ן שם
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">When Yaakov was asking his sons to bury him in the מערת המכפלה, he mentioned that Yitzchak was buried there to emphasize how special the place was to further invigorate his sons to make sure that they buried him there.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We are talking about the shevatim, and this is Yaakov's dying wish. We would surely imagine that they would try their utmost to fulfill it.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In addition, they probably already knew who was buried in the מערת המכפלה.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see that hearing something one more time, no matter who you are and in what situation you are in, can help galvanize one further to try and accomplish something.</span>
# Footnotes

[^1]: The Medrash actually says that Reuven was the first person do to תשובה in history, entirely. However, there were some people who had done תשובה earlier, like Adam. As such, rebbi understood the Medrash to be saying that this תשובה was done with a uniquely high degree of sincerity.
