# Shmuess 1
## &lrm;סוטה לד:, במדבר יג כב רש"י שם
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">כלב went to daven in חברון by the kivrei אבות to be saved from the plan of the מרגלים.
יהושע didn't go because משה had already davened for him to be saved when he changed his name from הושע to יהושע.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It sounds like יהושע would have davened by the kivrei אבות with כלב if not for the fact that משה already davened for him.
Why did כלב and יהושע need such תפילות? It is clear that they had already seen through the plan of the מרגלים. What were they concerned about?</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It seems that we see the power of the influence of our peers. כלב and יהושע were concerned that even though at that time they saw the clear truth, they would be convinced by the rest of the מרגלים.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Now, obviously, the מרגלים were tremendous גדולים and were certainly quite convincing, but we are also dealing with the giants like יהושע and כלב, and, even so, they were still concerned. We see the tremendous power of peer pressure.</span>
# Shmuess 2
## &lrm;מסילת ישרים פרק ה
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If someone's friends make fun of him for doing the right thing, the person should pay no heed to them and, in fact, should respond by making fun of them.
He should think to himself how he wouldn't give up a large bounty because people are making fun of him.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">דוד המלך discusses in תהלים how he stood strong against other kings that he would talk with. He wouldn't talk about תענוגות with them. Instead he would talk about Hashem and תורה, even though it was objectively embarrassing to be talking to other kings about such matters. דוד paid no heed to this.</span>
## Shmuess
### First Point
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is משמע that responding with cynicism to the harassers is actually a tactic that is necessary to help one do the right thing. It is clearly not easy to stand up to harassment and one needs to fight to be able to do so.</span>
### Second Point
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">דוד המלך was talking about his love and dedication to Hashem. Clearly, the fact that he didn't talk to these kings about the things that they would usually talk about was something that he found praiseworthy.
Why would דוד המלך find this praiseworthy? If one was in the company of children and they were talking about whatever they found interesting it does not seem like a particularly great accomplishment for one not to feel pressure to join in on their conversations.
Apparently, that is not the correct way to view things. This was a ניסיון (to some degree) for דוד. He was able to completely pass the ניסיון, but we still see how difficult it is for one to go against their peers.</span>
# Shmuess 3
## &lrm; במדבר טז א רש"י שם, גור אריה שם
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The פסוק says that קרח "took" something. It means that he "took" himself away from כלל ישראל. In other words, to get himself to be able to fight with משה, he had to view himself as if he was no longer part of כלל ישראל.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">קרח was a tremendous צדיק before his fight with משה. Clearly, there were powerful נגיעות that were affecting his decisions. Yet, it seems that if קרח viewed himself as a part of כלל ישראל, he would not have been able to get himself to fight with them and with משה.
There is a tremendous power to viewing oneself as a part of a group.
(This can be used in a positive way. If someone views himself as a part of the ישיבה, they will naturally feel a certain connection to the hashkafos and are more likely to stay in line with them. Also, see the following shmuess.)</span>
# Shmuess 4
## &lrm;ספר החינוך מצוה צה
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The reason for the מצוה of building a בית המקדש is so that people will have a place to go which is centered 100% around קדושה. This will have a positive effect on people when they come to be עולה רגל to act in this manner, and they will take the positive effects with them when they leave.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">While there are obviously positive mystical effects, as well, the ספר החינוך is telling us that there are other positive effects.
We can see from the חינוך that having a sanctuary which is totally dedicated to the רצון Hashem can be a particular force in helping one improve.
(Similar to what we just said in the previous shmuess, being part of ישיבה can give one this place. Obviously, the ישיבה is nothing like the level of קדושה of the בית המקדש, although, on the flip side, one who is part of the ישיבה is hopefully there more than just three times per year.
Additionally, this is the reason behind the השקפה of the ראש ישיבה to have certain standards in ישיבה, even if they were above the standards of the individual בני הישיבה. Having a place that is fully focused on the רצון Hashem is a powerful boost to everyone who is a part of it, and the stronger the focus is, the greater the impact.)</span>
# Shmuess 5
## &lrm;בראשית רבה סה ד (כו לה), עץ יוסף שם
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The fact that the wives of Esav brought קרבנות to עבודה זרות pained both יצחק and רבקא tremendously. However, because רבקא had been exposed to this when growing up, it bothered her slightly less.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This story happened over 60 years after רבקא had left the house where she grew up. She had spent that time growing as one of the אמהות, in the pure home that she built with יצחק.
Additionally, we aren't talking about strangers doing עבודה זרה, we are talking about her own daughter-in-laws.
Yet, despite this, there was still something there that made her less bothered.</span>

<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see how powerful negative impacts can be.</span>
