# &lrm;במדבר לב לב רבינו בחיי
בני גד בני ראובן told משה that they would cross the ירדן armed before Hashem (meaning, at the front of the army).
However, instead of saying the word "אנחנו" they said the word "נחנו" (in the context of "*we* will cross the ירדן before Hashem").
Even though both words mean the same thing and "אנחנו" is usually the word used for "we", they used the word "נחנו" before this is a לשון of more עניוות.
They used this lashen because they said that they will go at the front of the army, which implies that they were powerful warriors, which has tones of גאווה.
# Shmuess
First of all, for a few reasons, it seems that they was not meant in a gaava way. This is because:
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">משה used this same language of 'חלוץ לפני ה (a few פסוקים earlier in פסוק כט) when telling them that they would have to cross the ירדן and fight. בני גד בני ראובן were talking to משה and so they used his language.</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The reason they were going in front was because of this deal they made with משה, not necessarily because of their strength. This was the "price" for getting the land on the other side of the ירדן.</span>
Additionally, if the right thing is to use the word "נחנו", then that is the right thing. What does it have t do with saying "'לפני ה"? Is it some sort of tashlumin, since they were about to use a gaavadic לשון they first had to use a לשון of עניוות?

It seems that פשט is that one has to stay far away from bad מדות. Since the לשון of "'לפני ה" perhaps somewhat implies a level of גאווה, even in this situation that it doesn't imply that at all, one has to stay far away, and so they used a לשון of עניוות ("נחנו") to show (to themselves, presumably) that there was no גאווה here. We see that at least when it comes to גאווה (although presumably by other מדות, as well), for a person with the proper sensitivities, one needs to stay far away, even from things which are actually מותר.
# Additional Thoughts
I discussed this was Rabbi Avi Rhodes a bit. Rabbi Grunblatt may have dropped a line during the shmuess that they did this to show others that this was not about גאווה, but Rabbi Rhodes (and myself) didn't think that was Rabbi Grunblatt meant.
Rabbi Rhodes pointed out that while we usually say this concept by מצוות, over here we are even saying it about מדות.
Rabbi Rhodes also pointed out that the רמב"ם says that one needs to stay as far away from two מדות, &lrm;גאווה and anger. As such, we can't prove from this shmuess how far this idea goes (because we are especially concerned about vestiges of גאווה), but presumably, the concept is true about other מדות, as well.
