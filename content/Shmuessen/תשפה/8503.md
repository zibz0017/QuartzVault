# &lrm;בכור שור <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">I Don't Know where</span>
If someone is חייב סקילה, the עידים who witnessed the action and got this person convicted are the first people to throw stones.
The reason for this is because the rest of the people will be unsure if this person really was חייב מיתה. When they see that the עידים are really willing to take the step of pelting him with stones, that will reinforce their confidence in the פסק of בית דין.
# &lrm;גיטין <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">I Don't Know where</span>
People wouldn't falsely sign a document. However, they would go over to other people and tell them that a document was from the husband to get these people to sign.
# &lrm;מלכים א יח
By אליהו on הר הכרמל he wanted a cow to be offered by the priests of בעל. However, the cow didn't want to go. Eventually, אליהו was able to convince it that this was the correct thing to do. The cow agreed to go but only if אליהו would give it over.
# Shmuess
## Quesetion
In all of these scenarios, it seems that taking an action makes a large difference.
In the first situation, if the עידים were really lying, even if they didn't throw the first stone, they basically killed him. It doesn't appear that the fact that they threw the first stone should make much of a difference. Either way, they got this innocent person killed.
In the second scenario, this person is getting this false document to be written in the name of someone. What is the difference whether he does it himself  to he arranges for it to be done?
In the third example, if אליהו is wrong, the fact that the cow was improperly used for עבודה זרה was because of אליהו. What is the difference whether אליהו handed the cow over himself, or not?
## Answer
Apparently, taking a physical action makes a big difference. It shows to a surprisingly large extent that the person is buying in to what he is doing. This is even when there is no actual difference that comes out from this person doing the actual action, or just arranging for the action to be done.
