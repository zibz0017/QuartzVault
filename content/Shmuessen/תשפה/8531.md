# Shmuess 1
## ריב"א (ריב"א) on ויקרא כ יט (Explaining the אבן עזרא)
 The תורה does not discuss the punishments for living with one's aunt (father or mother's sister) or two sisters. This is out of כבוד for כלל ישראל for which violations of these הלכות played significant roles in its history. יעקב married two sisters and Amram married his aunt Yocheved.

## Nefesh החיים Shaar 1
*Note: I am not sure if it was shaar 1*

Even before the תורה was given, there is tremendous good that is brought to the world by keeping the תורה. יעקב and Amram understood the importance of keeping the הלכות but they also knew that despite that, it was still even more important for them to marry the people that they did. 

Obviously, once the תורה is given then one can't make such decisions. One is bound by the תורה.

This is partly why the תורה was only given later, after the creation of כלל ישראל, namely, that Amram and יעקב would not have been able to marry the people that they did once the תורה was given, even though it was vital that Amram and יעקב married the people that they did.
## Shmuess
1. It doesn't seem that mentioning the punishment that one gets for marrying these relatives is a much of a gnai.
2. Its not as if the whole איסור is not mentioned. The איסור is still written in the תורה. We still know that it is an ervah.
3. Its not like we don't know the punishment. We learn them out from drashos.
4. As mentioned in the Nefesh החיים, this was actually the correct thing for יעקב and Amram to do.
Despite this, כלל ישראל would still feel that gnai and so it is removed from the תורה to spare כלל ישראל from feeling that pain. We see the level of sensitivity that people have.
# Shmuess 2
## Background
A man cursed Hashem. כלל ישראל didn't know what his punishment for that was and so they asked משה and משה asked Hashem. While that was happening, the man was placed under watch.

At the same time that this was happening, there was a different person who gathered sticks on שבת. The punishment for him was death. He was also kept under watch until his sentence was meted out. <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">I am unsure what they were waiting for but it seems irrelevant to the shmuess.</span>
## רש"י on ויקרא כד יב (as explained by the שפתי Chachomim)
The man who cursed Hashem (the mekalel) was kept under watch in a separate place from the person had gathered the sticks (the mekosheish eitzim). This is because they didn't want the mekalel to think that he was chayiv misah and cause him pain when they didn't know that this was the case.
## Shmuess
1. Keeping the mekosheish and the mekalel together doesn't actually mean anything. The reason why they would do it is obvious: it takes less people to watch two people together than separately.
   It seems that this person would have still made this connection even though there really was no logical reason to make one.
2. We can just tell the mekalel exactly what is happening. Tell him that it was not yet known what the הלכה for him is and they were in the middle of figuring it out.
   Apparently, the mekalel would not have believed them.
3. This person is on the lowest of madreigos. He cursed Hashem. This does not seem to be the type of person that we should have to do any special arrangements for.
4. The reason why the mekalel would potentially not get misah is because he may not deserve the kapara that one gets for getting misah. If anything, this person should be hopeful that he receives the same fate as the mekosheish.
   It seems that he would not have thought about this, or, at most, he would only partially think about this, but still partially be fearful of getting killed.
5. Its not effortless to watch two people separately. It takes more people to watch them, now, rather than putting them together.
We see the lengths that we need to go to care about the feelings of others. Even people on the lowest of madreigos.
# Shmuess 3
## רש"י on שמות כא לז
Someone who steals a sheep only needs to pay back 4 times the value (rather than 5 times, like one would pay for an ox) because the thief had to carry the sheep through the streets which is embarrassing. As such, he as alread received some of the punishment that he deserved.
## Shmuess
1. If we would ask the thief how he feels, he would obviously say that he is happy with the fact that he has the sheep.
2. This person is obviously not a very good person. He is a thief.
3. We charge this person a whole sheep less. That seems to be at least a somewhat significant sum of significant amount of money. As such, it doesn't seem to only be some dakei dakos of bushah that the person feels.[^1]
Apparently, even a thief feels this bushah, even though at the exact same time, he is feeling happy that he id getting away with the sheep. (Rebbi said that the Rosh Yeshiva would say that this is even on a conscious level, not just subconscious.) We see how sensitive people are and how much we have to care about that.
# Own Thoughts
The way that rebbi presented all three shmuessen was that the person who is experiencing the bushah or gnai actually feels it. While I certainly hear that for the second shmuess (the mekalel feeling nervous about being kept in custody with the mekosheish), I could have heard that in the first shmuess (the person feeling the gnai that significant parts of כלל ישראל were made with relationships that would eventually be אסור) and third shmuess (the thief feeling the bushah from carrying the sheep), the person does not actually feel the bushah, but that it is "bietzem" a gnai, regardless of whether the person feels it.[^2] We would still learn the lesson that we need to be careful with how we treat others, but we would not necessarily be able to see the level of sensitivity one has.

[^1]: I don't believe that rebbi said this ציור but it seems to be true.
[^2]: I have a hard time defining this to myself, but I believe that this is something that we relate to. For example, when dealing with children with a high level of special needs, or older babies/young toddlers, we feel that it is proper to treat them with dignity even if the child his/herself doesn't feel it. It seems wrong not to.
