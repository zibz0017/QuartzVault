# Shmuess 1
## &lrm;ספר החינוך מצוה שיא
There are four things a person should do when preparing for Rosh Hashana:
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Tzedakah.</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Daven.</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Change one's name.</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Change one's actions.</span>
Changing one's name gets one to think of himself as a different person and helps them turn over a new leaf.
## Shmuess
Changing one's name is superficial. It's not anything of real substance. It shouldn't seem to make such a difference. In addition, it is put with tzedakah, davening, and changing one's actions, which are all obviously very legitimate things to do. Clearly changing one's name is also rather legitimate.

We see how much we are affected by even superficial things. This apparently has the potential to make a difference.
# Shmuess 2
## &lrm;מלכים ב פרק יב
## Background
Elisha did a neis for someone Naaman. Naaman thanked Hashem and wanted to give Elisha something for being the conduit of Hashem's neis. Elisha refused to accept the gift.
## &lrm;רלב"ג
*Note: In the תועליות, I think. I don't know which one.*
This was because accepting the gift would make it seem that Elisha did the neis, not Hashem. This would ruin the purpose of the neis which was to increase אמונה in Hashem.
## Shmuess
We see the power of superficial appearances. Naaman just thanked Hashem for doing the neis. It does seem appropriate to give Elisha something for being the conduit for the neis. Yet, despite this, paying Elisha appears to acknowledging that Elisha did the neis and that would have an effect.

(Rebbi might have also added that the לשון of the רלב"ג is rather strong. He makes it seem that nearly the whole effect of the neis would be ruined by this.)
# Shmuess 3
## &lrm;שמות י כה חזקוני ד"ה גם אתה
פרעה thought that the darkness of Makas Chochesh permeated the whole מצרים, including where the Jews lived. As such, he thought that tefillah would not help.
## &lrm;שמות י כג חזקוני ד"ה היה אור
In truth, it was light for כלל ישראל wherever they went.
## Shmuess
Why did פרעה assume this? It seems that he thought so because this is the way things appeared to him. Everything appeared dark to him, so he assumed it was dark for everyone else.

Obviously, פרעה didn't want the chochesh, for himself, personally, and for his nation. And there wasn't actually any reason to assume that just because it was dark for him that it would be dark for the Jews. The previous makkos had certainly shown that the Mitzri'im could be affected by a makkah that the Jews were not. Despite all this, פרעה didn't see past his own assumption.

We see how appearance can have tremendous power.
# Shmuess 4
## &lrm;במדבר ו יג רבינו יונה ד"ה ביום מלאת
A nazir brings a korban when he finishes his nezirus, not because he did anything wrong, but because he is now ending his nezirus and so there is an appearance of "stepping down". This appearance may affect him negatively. As such, he brings a korban to try and negate this affect.
## Shmuess
This person acted properly by becoming a nazir. He went beyond the letter of the law. It is now the right time for him to go back to normal. Despite that, the appearance of going back to normal may affect him.
We see the power of superficial appearances.
