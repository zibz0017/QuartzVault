# Shmuess 1
## &lrm;במדבר יא יז רבינו בחיי ד"ה ואצלתי מן
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Hashem took some of His שפע from משה רבינו and gave it to some of the zikainim who would lead with משה. משה's greatness was heightened through this as משה now became more like Hashem. Hashem has His "בית דין" that receives שפע from Hashem, and now משה has his "בית דין" (70 זקנים) that receives שפע from משה.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">For us, this seems to be lowering משה's status. משה used to be head and shoulders above everyone else. Now he has "competition".</span>

<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see how the תורה views things. Greatness is not measured against others. It is measured by how similar one is to Hashem.</span>

<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">(In the context of the topic, the idea was that one should not compare themselves to others as this is not what the תורה considers to be important.)</span>
# Shmuess 2
## &lrm;בבא קמא ג: תוס' ד"ה כדמתרגם רב יוסף
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">רב יוסף would always say the תרגום of פסוקים instead of the actual words. Some say that he was blind and so he was unable to see the words. As such, he had to say the תרגום as he was not allowed to say the actual words (one is not allowed to say תורה שבכתב by heart[^1]).</span>

<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">However, this can't be. There is a concept of 'עת לעשות לה (as we see from .גיטין ס) which is that at a time when a tremendous learning of תורה would be lost, one can "violate" הלכה and do things which would otherwise not be permitted. If רב יוסף was actually blind, this case would be the greatest case of 'עת לעשות לה and so רב יוסף would certainly be allowed to say the actual פסוקים, even though he was not reading them.</span>
## &lrm;גיטין ס.
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">*Note: I am not sure if I have this exactly correct, but the basic idea and how it relates to the shmuess is correct.*</span>

<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There is a הלכה that one is not allowed to learn from a נ"ך that is not complete. However, there was a time that they didn't have completed נ"ךs and so people simply didn't lein haftorahs. This caused a loss of תורה. As such, Reish Lakish and Rav Yochanan paskened that it was an 'עת לעשות לה and permitted people to lein from נ"ךs that were not complete.</span>
## &lrm;תוס' ד"ה כדמתרגם רב יוסף בב"ק ג:
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">*Background: The 'גמ in ב"ק quotes מתרגם רב יוסף who gives the תרגום of a פסוק*</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">רב יוסף was an expert on תרגום, so the 'גמ refers to his תרגום.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Some מפרשים learn that רב יוסף said the תרגום of פסוקים over because he was blind and so he was not allowed to say the actual פסוקים. However, this can't be because if רב יוסף was actually blind, he would certainly be allowed to say the פסוקים even though he could not see them because he obviously has no way of seeing the פסוקים, and so this is the ultimate 'עת לעשות לה. ('עת לעשות לה is discussed in .גיטין ס, [[#&lrm;גיטין ס.|above]].)</span>
## &lrm;או"ח סי' מט מ"ב ס"ק א
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One who is blind or is in prison without access to sefarim may say the פסוקים, even though he is not reading them. (The source for this הלכה is the [[#&lrm;תוס' ד"ה כדמתרגם רב יוסף בב"ק ג|aformentioned 'תוס]].)</span>
## Shmuess
### Question
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">How does 'תוס think he can bring a proof from .גיטין ס to רב יוסף? In .גיטין ס we were talking about a national issue and רב יוסף was just one individual. As such, perhaps we are willing to invoke 'עת לעשות לה in .גיטין ס, but not for  רב יוסף? To emphasize the point, 'תוס isn't giving an answer and proposing that *perhaps* the gemaros are comparable, he is going on offense and bringing a proof from that 'גמ to the הלכה regarding רב יוסף.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Furthermore, it wasn't that רב יוסף couldn't learn at all. He was able to learn using the תרגום of the פסוק. Obviously the תרגום was quite accurate because it was his own תרגום (and he was an amorah). He could even think the פסוקים in his head. How can 'תוס bring a proof from .גיטין ס to רב יוסף when רב יוסף's תורה learning seemingly isn't heavily affected?</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Not only this, but the מ"ב paskens like this 'תוס.</span>
### Answer
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It seems that we need to say that we see the importance of every individual. 'תוס clearly doesn't think that the fact that רב יוסף is just an individual and .גיטין ס is dealing with the masses makes a difference. The תורה of every person makes a big difference, even if it is only just a little bit more, but every little bit makes a big difference, enough to be equivalent to the תורה of the masses. 'תוס was so confident that he was willing to bring a proof from the masses to the individual, and he even called it "אין לך עת לעשות גדול מזו".</span>
# Shmuess 3
## &lrm;רלב"ג תועליות תועלת ד על פרשת בחוקותי
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The signal for all of the בני ישראל to convene was one pattern for all of the בני ישראל at once, rather than doing it for different shevatim individually. This was because had it been done for the individual shevatim, it would have to be done in an order, and people would think that there was a reason for the order and they would be angry that they weren't called earlier, as they would take this as a sign of disrespect.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see how naturally competitive we are, even when it doesn't make sense. We are talking about the דור דעה. And if the blowings were done for individual shevatim, then there would have to be *some* order. It didn't have to mean anything at all. Yet people would naturally interpret it that way, and for this reason, Hashem set up the system to avoid this issue.</span>
# Shmuess 4
## &lrm;במדבר כ ב רלב"ג ד"ה ולא היה
Miriam, using her chachma was able to positively more כלל ישראל toward avodas Hashem. The תורה puts the parsha of כלל ישראל complaining about the lack of water right after her death as if to say that כלל ישראל would not have complained had Miriam still been around.
## &lrm;רלב"ג תועליות תועלת ד על פרשת חקת
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The death of צדיקים are extremely, as we see by the power effects of Miriam's and Aharon's death. This is even though משה was still alive at the time that they died.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Sometimes we think that since we are not the best, we aren't able to make much of a difference. That is not the case, as we see here.</span>


# Shmuess 5
## &lrm;מדרש תנחומא ויקהל ז
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A certain wealthy lady asked Rebbi Yosi why is it that Hashem gives wisdom to the wise? Wouldn't it make more sense for Hashem to give wisdom to the fool? After all, he is the one who needs it.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rebbi Yosi responded by asking the woman if she was going to lend money to one of two people, one rich, and one poor, who would she lend money to?</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The woman responded that she would lend money to the rich person as  she can be more assured that she would get her money back.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rebbi Yosi responded that the same was true about Hashem giving out wisdom. If Hashem would give wisdom to the fool, he would just go and waste it. The wise person utilizes his wisdom to learn תורה and serves Hashem with it.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">*I wrote this shmuess down much later and I am not sure I got it right, although I do think I did.*</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see our tremendous power. If we waste our wisdom and don't learn תורה it is like Hashem is missing something, כביכול (the same way that the woman would have lost out on her money). And the opposite is true as well. When we do learn, we are "giving" Hashem something that he wouldn't have had otherwise, כביכול.</span>
# Shmuess 6

<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Everyone has their own path in life because everyone is unique and no two people are alike. It used to be that one would go to a נביא and ask him what he should do with his life. Now that we lost נבואה, there is רוח הקודש instilled in every Jew and a person can feel what they are supposed to do.</span>
## Shmuess

<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see how unique every person is and that every person has a unique mission.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As a disclaimer, one should still make sure to ask shailos to one's rabbeim regarding hadracha. It is difficult to know how to apply this idea that everyone has רוח הקודש and an inate sense of what they should do.</span>
# Own Thoughts
## On Shmuess 2
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">What does it mean that the individual outweighs the masses? What is the masses if not a bunch of individuals? As such, why would value the תורה of one individual equal to that of the masses?</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Chaim Shotkin thought that you are going to have to add some details to the story. It seems that the תורה that רב יוסף would have lost was greater in some way than the תורה masses.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One thing to note is that רב יוסף was an amorah. It is quite reasonable to assume that the תורה he was learning was on a far deeper level than that of other people. Especially considering the fact that we are talking about listening to the haftorah. It is reasonable that the masses didn't delve into the haftorah anywhere as deeply as רב יוסף delved into his learning. As such, something which messes with the deep תורה of רב יוסף may be more impactful than that of the masses.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Additionally, people could have learned some other way. It is not known whether they chose to or not, but the fact that they had other options to learn while רב יוסף did not would also seem to increase the level of 'עת לעשות לה for רב יוסף versus the masses.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">These points would perhaps minimize the shmuess, but it is still a big חידוש, that the learning of one individual, even a great individual, and even if his learning isn't so strongly affected, can outweigh the learning of the masses.</span>
