# Shmuess 1
## &lrm;בראשית לג יג ספורנו שם
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">יעקב was telling עשו that they couldn't travel together because יעקב would try to keep pace with עשו, out of honor for עשו and יעקב's children and his flocks wouldn't be able to handle it.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">יעקב told עשו that he couldn't allow this because it was his responsibility to care for them.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">*I wrote this over later so I don't remember it well. I believe that rebbi was learning the ספורנו to hold that יעקב was telling עשו that his kids would die if he pushed them, but I am not sure if I am remembering correctly.*</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">יעקב is trying to convince עשו that they can't travel together. What is the argument he uses to try and make this point? He tells עשו about responsibility.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">What about the fact that his kids are going to die? Let's say יעקב wasn't responsible for their wellbeing, would that change anything? Of course יעקב couldn't push his children too hard, regardless of who was responsible. Why didn't he just say that to עשו?
Apparently, there is something about responsibility that can resonate with עשו even more deeply than the wellbeing of יעקב's children.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">We see how natural and powerful the feeling of responsibility is.</span>
# Shmuess 2
## &lrm;בראשית לב כו ספורנו שם
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">יעקב was fighting the malach, and because of יעקב's extreme diveikus to Hashem, the malach was unable to hurt יעקב. To be able to shake this diveikus, he showed יעקב a time when the leaders of כלל ישראל would cause כלל ישראל to sin.[^1] This made יעקב afraid and it broke his concentration for a moment. The malach took advantage and hurt יעקב.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There were many things that the malach was able to show יעקב. He could have showed him the עגל, or the Crusades, or the Holocaust, or many other great עבירות and times that כלל ישראל suffered. What did he choose? He chose the time that the leaders would fail the nation. Clearly this was a particularly powerful. Why was this particularly powerful?</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It seems that the leadership is of vital importance, and when even the leadership can't come through, כלל ישראל is in a really bad place. This is even compared to the other events mentioned. In these other events, although כלל ישראל may have done terrible things or may have been suffering tremendously, there was more hope for their recovery. Without leaders, though, כלל ישראל was in an extremely precarious situation.</span>
# Shmuess 3
## &lrm;בראשית רבה מא ח (יג יא) עץ יוסף ד"ה וללוט אחיו
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">אברהם sent לוט away too harshly. Particularly in light of how accepting he was of all people, and here, for his relative, he was overly harsh, he was particularly held to task.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If אברהם had been less harsh then perhaps לוט may have come back.</span>
## Shmuess
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">*I wrote this over much later and I am very unsure if I am remembering this correctly.*</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As the מדרש implies, אברהם was the "pioneer" of קירוב. And as the מדרש says, we are talking about אברהם's relative, who of course he loved and wanted the best for. Of course אברהם would have loved for לוט to do תשובה. Yet because he was overly harsh when sending him away (obviously on a דקי דקות level), this was partially the reason that לוט never did תשובה.</span>
<span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If אברהם could make a mistake in such a situation when dealing with a relative whom he loved, how much more so are we prone to making a mistake in a similar situation when deciding to send a student away or something similar. And even when we are doing the right thing by sending the student away, we see how important doing it in the correct way is.</span>
