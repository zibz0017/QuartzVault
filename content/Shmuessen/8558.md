# Shmuess 1
## במדבר יא יז רבינו בחיי ד"ה ואצלתי מן
Hashem took some of His שפע from Moshe Rabbeinu and gave it to some of the zikainim who would lead with Moshe. Moshe's greatness was heightened through this as Moshe now became more like Hashem. Hashem has His "beis din" that receives שפע from Hashem, and now Moshe has his "beis din" (70 zikeinim) that receives שפע from Moshe.
## Shmuess
For us, this seems to be lowering Moshe's status. Moshe used to be head and shoulders above everyone else. Now he has "competition".

We see how the Torah views things. Greatness is not measured against others. It is measured by how similar one is to Hashem.

(In the context of the topic, the idea was not not compare oneself to others as this is not what the Torah considers to be important.)
# Shmuess 2
## בבא קמא ג: תוס' ד"ה כדמתרגם רב יוסף
רב יוסף would always say the targum of passukim instead of the actual words. Some say that he was blind and so he was unable to see the words. As such, he had to say the targum as he was not allowed to say the actual words (one is not allowed to say תורה שבכתב by heart[^1]).

However, this can't be. There is a concept of 'עת לעשות לה (as we see from .גיטין ס) which is that at a time when a tremendous learning of Torah would be lost, one can "violate" halacha and do things which would otherwise not be permitted. If רב יוסף was actually blind, this case would be the greatest case of 'עת לעשות לה and so רב יוסף would certainly be allowed to say the actual passukim, even though he was not reading them.
## .גיטין ס
*Note: I am not sure if I have this exactly correct, but the basic idea and how it relates to the shmuess is correct.*

There is a halacha that one is not allowed to learn from a נ"ך that is not complete. However, there was a time that they didn't have completed נ"ךs and so people simply didn't lein haftorahs. This caused a loss of Torah. As such, Reish Lakish and Rav Yochanan paskened that it was an 'עת לעשות לה and permitted people to lein from נ"ךs that were not complete.
# Footnotes

[^1]: There are caveats to this halacha. None of those caveats apply here, but research this halacha and/or speak to a rav before paskening like this.
