
# Basic Storyline With Minimal Peirush
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Shevet אפרים angry at Yiftach and so they confronted him.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">אפרים was so incensed that they threatened to burn down Yiftach's house.[^2]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach responded that he had asked them in the past and they had not come. In addition, he had been victorious without them.[^3]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Fighting broke out between Yiftach and his men against אפרים.[^4]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach's men guarded all of the bridges over the Yarden and whenever someone would come by they would compel the person to say a word that people from אפרים would not be able to pronounce properly (then would pronounce an "sh" sound like an "s").[^5]</span>
6. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If the person was proven to be from shevet אפרים, he would be killed.[^6]</span>
7. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In all 42,000 from shevet אפרים were killed.[^6]</span>
# Storyline as Discussed by the מפרשים
## What was אפרים Angry About
### Yiftach Fought Without Them
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Gideon, a previous shofet, had also fought against the enemies of כלל ישראל without אפרים, and they were insulted the, as well.[^7] The fact that now it happened again compounded the insult.[^8]</span>
### Yiftach was Chosen as Leader Without Their Imput
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">אפרים was upset because they felt that Gilad was a relatively unimportant part of shevet יוסף.[^9][^10][^11] This was because they were either entirely across the Yarden (which only had half of Menashe, as opposed to the ארץ ישראל side which had the other half of Menashe plus the entire אפרים)[^11] or they were actually divided: half of Gilad was across the Yarden in shevet Menashe's land, and half were in ארץ ישראל, in shevet אפרים's land.[^10] As such, they did not feel that Gilad had the right to choose the leader.[^10][^12]</span> ^a260b0
## How אפרים Insulted Yiftach
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The simple reading of the פסוק is that אפרים threatened to burn down Yiftach's house with him inside.[^2] (The מצודת דוד learns the פסוק this way.[^13])</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to the מנחה קטנה, אפרים only threatened to destroy Yiftach's house, but not with him inside. Yiftach's house (which was presumably nice as he was the shofet), was an honor for him. By burning down Yiftach's house, and attacking his honor through the destruction of something physical, אפרים hoped Yiftach would learn that all the more so, he should care about the honor of humans (meaning, the honor of אפרים).[^14]</span>
3. Additionally, אפרים called Gilad an unimportant part of shevet יוסף, [[#^a260b0|as discussed]].
## Could אפרים Have Been Appeased
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to some מפרשים, אפרים came in with the intent to fight and were not going to accept an apology. This was because Gideon had similarly not included them, and then apologized afterwards. The fact that it happened again showed אפרים that the apology was insincere and they were not going to be willing to accept one now.[^14]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The מלבי"ם says that Yiftach responded gently but אפרים didn't listen.[^11]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to other מפרשים, Yiftach could have and should have attempted to appease אפרים.[^15] There מפרשים would seem to hold that Yiftach's response was not so apologetic.</span>
## Yiftach's Response
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Amon largely (perhaps entirely) focused in the people of Gilad (Yiftach's extended family).[^16][^17]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach had asked them for assistance in other battles and they had not come[^18][^17] in which case the hypocrisy that they hadn't come to fight against Amon and are only coming now to fight Yiftach is glaringly aparent.[^19]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach had asked for their assistance in this battle, as well, but Hashem prevented them from coming because Hashem didn't want them to take part in the victory. It was clear from this, and the fact that Yiftach had won in miraculous fashion (he had won with a small army) that clearly Hashem would not be on their side if they fought against him.[^16]</span>
	1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to this, I don't even begin to understand what אפרים was upset about. What did they mean that Yiftach didn't invite them if Yiftach, in fact, did? Perhaps the message was lost on the way, or only arrived there after the battle was over?</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">אפרים had ample opportunity to save כלל ישראל as Amon had been antagonizing כלל ישראל for years. If they were ever going to do it, they had their chance.[^20]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach told אפרים that he had been kicked out from his family and that had turned him into a humble person. As such, he did not leave אפרים out because he thought he was so great he didn't need them or anything like that.[^21]</span>
6. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach pointed out that he had risked his life, and he had won through only with a נס&lrm;[^22][^20] (he only had a small army against Amon[^22]).</span>
7. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach told אפרים that if he had lost, אפרים would have a reasonable: perhaps if he would have included אפרים in his army he would have won. But now that he was victorious without אפרים, their complaint was unjustified.[^23]</span>
## Who From אפרים Fought
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to numerous מפרשים, the entire shevet אפרים was involved in this fight.[^24][^14]</span>
	1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">I am confused by the מנחה קטנה who says that the whole shevet fought, but then over [[#^51c48b|here]] implies that it was only some of the shevet. Perhaps he means that a very high percentage fought, and the מנחה קטנה just calls it the whole shevet, even though it wasn't quite everyone?</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The רד"ק says that it was the "רקים והפחותים" of אפרים.[^25]</span>
## Ascertaining Who was Guilty
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At the river crossings that Yiftach's men were guarding, they would ask the travelers what shevet they were from. If they would answer that they were from אפרים, then Yiftach's men would look into the matter somewhat, and ultimately let them go. If they had nothing to hide then that would show that they were not guilty.[^26]</span> ^51c48b
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If they would say that they were from some from a shevet other than אפרים and then they would not be able to make the "sh" sound, that would show that they lied, which would indicate their guilt.[^26]</span>
## Responsibility for the Tragedy
### אפרים's Responsibility

1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This was a punishment for עבירות that אפרים had done in secrecy.[^25]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">אפרים should have been grateful to Yiftach for having gotten rid of the enemies of כלל ישראל and risked his life to do so. Not only were they not grateful, but they were even antagonistic.[^15]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This was a punishment for Avimelech, an evil shofet of כלל ישראל, who's followers and base of operations were in Shichem. This was a city in shevet אפרים's territory, and they should have protested Avimelech's actions. They were punished for not doing so.[^14]</span>
### Yiftach's Responsibility
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At the same time, Yiftach should have tried appeasing them as Gideon had done earlier in a similar circumstance.[^27][^15]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach died in a horrible way (see [[#^6b4328|here]]), but the מדרש (and the מפרשים presenting the מדרש) say that that was a punishment for not being מתיר נדר on his daughter. Almost nobody says that it was at least partically a punishment for killing אפרים.[^28]</span>
