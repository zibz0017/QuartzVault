# Breads that the גזרה Applies to
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The גזרה only applies to regular bread that a person where there is a concern that the person will eat it with dairy/meat.</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The גזרה does not apply to things like sweet bread, for which there is no concern that one will eat with dairy/meat.</span>
   (What about something like cream cheese frosting? Is there concern that someone will eat this with the sweet bread? Also, what is meant by sweet bread? Is sweet bread a pastry, or more "bread-like"? The ט"ז somewhere said that there is unique concern by bread because bread is eaten with everything. I don't know if that would apply to random pastries.)
# When One is Allowed to Eat Dairy/Meat Bread
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">For breads that the גזרה applies to, one may make and eat dairy/meat bread when:</span>
	1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If the bread is somewhat small (exact amount is not said but it seems to be not more than a meal or two),</span>
	2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">or if the bread is made in a unique way.</span>
# Exceptions to the Exceptions
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">These previous rules are only true when:</span>
	1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This was done before it was baked. It does not help to cut bread into a unique shape after you bake it, for example.</span>
	2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">And this is only true for private use, not if you are selling the bread (or perhaps giving it out, but that is not particularly discussed).</span>
