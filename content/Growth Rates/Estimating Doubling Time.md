# Rule of 70
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A good way to estimate how long it will take for a for an amount to double at a certain annual rate of growth is to divide the 70 by the rate of growth.[^1]</span>
   <span style="color:rgb(112, 48, 160)">Example:</span> If you invest $1000 into an investment with a growth rate of 3% a year, it will take 23.4 years for the investment to be worth $2000 (see chart [[#^b6cf6b|below]]). If you estimated using the rule of 70, you would simply divide 70 by 3, which equals 23.3 (70 / 3 = 23.3), which, while not exact, is quite close.
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This estimate is quite accurate for growth rates 5% and under (and is spot-on for 2%), but less as the growth rate increases, as can be seen from this chart:[^1]</span>
   ![[Pasted image 20241030223920.png]] ^b6cf6b
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">You can add about .2 - .3 years to the estimate to help with the estimate.</span>

# Rule of 70 vs Rule of 72
## Advantages to the Rule of 70
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">70 is a decently easy number to divide by in your head as it is a multiple of ten.</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is quite accurate for rates 5% and below.</span>
## Advantages to the Rule of 72
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">72 divides by quite a few numbers perfectly, such as 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, and 12, making calculations for these growths rates quite easy.</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is quite accurate for growth rates between 2% and 15%.</span>
