# Saving and Borrowing Interest Rates
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Saving and borrowing follows the law of supply and demand, just like anything else.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">When the interest rate is high, people save more and borrow less. When the interest rate is low, people save less and borrow more.[^1]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The interest rate is determined by the intersection between the saving curve and the borrowing curve.[^1]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If there is a shift in one of the curves, that shifts the point of intersection.[^1]</span>

# How Money Goes from Savers to Borrowers
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Practically, most of the time, borrowers and savers do not actually meet. There almost always is a middleman, which is generally a bank.[^2]</span>
## How Banks Work
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Savers will deposit money in a bank and the bank will lend it to borrowers.[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Banks will pay savers interest on the money that they deposit at the bank and will charge interest to people who borrow the money.[^2]
   The bank's profit is the difference between how much they pay to savers and how much they collect from borrowers.[^2]</span>
## Why Banks Are Useful
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Banks link savers and borrowers.[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Because the bank lends to many people, they can afford to take smart risks. For example, if a private person was lending another private person money, the lender may be worried about lending the money if there was a 1% chance that the borrower would default on the loan (if it was a significant amount of money).
   Because banks are so large and lend to so many people, they can afford to take these smart risks and allow the law of averages to play out.[^2]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The bank does the research on borrowers to know if they are trustworthy or not. It would be very time consuming for individuals to do this on their own, and they probably wouldn't be as effective as the bank at it. Banks have experience in researching prospective borrowers as well as understanding how risky different types of loans are (a business loan versus a car loan versus a mortgage).[^2]</span>
