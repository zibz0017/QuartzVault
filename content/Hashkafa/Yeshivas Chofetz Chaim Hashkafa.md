## Importance of Saying Chaburos
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Saying Chaburos is really important for one's growth.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Saying a chabura forces one to push himself to the full extent of his abilities. The more one does this, the more they grow.[^1]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In addition, saying chaburos give a certain pressure to have things clear, because if you do not, the people you are saying the chabura over to will tear into you.[^1]</span>
## Pilpul Shiurim
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Pilpul shiurim are very difficult. The growth comes from the difficulty.[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Even if one "drops out" before the שיעור is over, the growth one gets from working and struggling to get whatever one was able to get from the pilpul שיעור makes it worth it to have them, despite all of the early dropping out.[^2]</span>
### General Advice
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Speak to your fellow talmidim and ask them your questions and to explain steps. Don't just try to only get things from the rebbi.[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As mentioned before, one should keep in mind the fact that the growth comes from the struggle.[^2]</span>
## Seder Hayom in Chevron Yeshiva
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The seder hayom in chevron yeshiva under Rav Meir Chadash was very similar to חפץ חיים's current seder hayom (albeit, they were greater than us).[^3]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In Chevron Yeshiva, morning seder was עיון and afternoon and night seder, were bekiyos.[^4]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One was expected to cover 4 to 5 blatt a week with רש"י, 'תוס, and some רא"ש and ר"ן.&lrm;[^4]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Meir Chadash was not happy with the bochurim who only learned עיון and no bekiyos.&lrm;[^4]</span>

# Gashmius
## Setting Limits
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One needs to have limits on gashmius. This is because gahmius is the antithesis of תורה and causes the תורה not to have an affect on a person. These is the famous shmuz of Shevna Hasofer who was a tremendous תלמיד chochom, but because of his attachment to gashmius, he ended up going off of the דרך, and the reason for this was because the attachment to gashmius did not allow the תורה to penetrate and change him.[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Once someone gets used to a certain level of gashmius it is difficult to give that up and "go down" a level. As such, increasing one's level of gashius is not some short-term commitment. It may make a difference down the road.[^2]</span>
## Wealth Vs Gashmius
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">To some degree, the wealthier one gets, the more gashmius they can have. However, one still needs to have limits because of the reasons discussed above.[^2]</span>
# Government Programs
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There is a negative to being a taker and taking government programs.[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">That being said, there is a balance that needs to be made. If one is going to have to leave yeshiva, or if one will stay but there will be financial stress, or if a mother is going to have to work a second job and have less time for her kids, it would seem reasonable to accept government programs or to take a pay cut to be able to qualify. One should keep in mind that this is not ideal, though.[^2] (I assume that  this does not mean that if someone feels depressed or something about taking government programs, they should keep this in mind. I assume the roshei yeshiva were referring to the average person who does not think that there are any negatives at all to doing this.)</span>
# Harbatzas התורה
## Motivating Thoughts
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One needs to think about the fact that for every person they can be mekarev (especially if the person was not frum to start, but even if they were already frum and they grew) they can have many descendants who are frum, or at least greater than they would have been, all because of what you did with one person.[^1]</span>
## Idealism About "Doing Something Big"
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">People are very idealistic about starting a branch or becoming a rosh yeshiva when leaving yeshiva. While obviously there is good in this, something to keep in mind is that there are so many ways for one to contribute.[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">People think that projects have a better chance of success than they actually do. In reality, almost no project seems to look promising when it starts.[^2]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">On the flip side, some of the very successful projects (like Miami, for example) had major struggles before turning into the successes that they are today.[^2]</span>

# White Shirts
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The Rosh Yeshiva felt that people should wear white shirts because of כבוד הבריות. However the Rosh Yeshiva was concerned that wearing white shirts could lead to gaavah. As such, the yeshiva didn't push it.[^8]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The yeshiva world has shifted toward wearing white shirts. Rabbi Harris felt that this wasn't due to feelings of כבוד הבריות, but rather because people simply don't want to be weird. (To me it sounded like rebbi was saying that if one feels the כבוד הבריות, one should wear a white shirt, but most people aren't holding there).[^8]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This doesn't mean that one shouldn't wear white shirts if one isn't doing it because of כבוד הבריות. There certainly is a סברא not to stand out and be weird.[^8]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If we would realize that everyone around us is just a prop and it is only us and Hashem, we wouldn't feel embarrassed. However, it is certainly not the norm for people to so fully דהר this and it is perfectly fine to feel uncomfortable for being so different.[^8]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Additionally, it sounded like rebbi thought that the gaava סברא wasn't such a strong סברא, especially בזמן הזה that everyone wears white shirts.[^8]</span>
6. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Guys suggested that the white shirts had come from chassidim and had "infiltrated" the litvishe world. Rabbi Harris disagreed. Rebbi said that when he was growing up, the non-Jewish workers (in white-collared jobs) used to wear white shirts.[^8]</span>
7. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">(On a side note, there has been a tremendous ירידת הדורות over time. Rabbi Harris remembers that the Rosh Yeshiva was surprised to hear that 9th graders didn't wear jackets in the streets.[^8])</span>
8. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The blue shirts are an impediment to others not from out yeshiva from being able to accept our yeshiva and gain from the מעלות that we have to offer.[^8]</span>
