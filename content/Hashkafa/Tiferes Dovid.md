# &lrm;דרך הלימוד
## Overarching Goal
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The main point is for one to put all of his efforts into trying to understand pshat in the ראשונים and try to tease out everything that the ראשונים are saying.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Saying "big chiddushim" or "geshmake תורה" is definitely not the goal.[^1]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There is a particular focus on trying to understand 'תוס.&lrm;[^1]</span>
## General Points
### Extracting from חז"ל
#### Main Idea
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There is more written "between the lines" than what is actually "on the line".[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One develops his understanding of the sugya through intense focus on extracting from what is written, rather than coming up with one's own svaros. The advantage of this is that one's תורה is built on חז"ל, rather than oneself.[^3]</span>
#### Particular Points to Examine
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">לשון.&lrm;[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">"כלומר"s in רש"י (רש"י isn't saying the פשוט פשט. What is the פשוט פשט and why didn't רש"י like it?)[^2]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">&lrm;המשך in 'תוס (what does a question have to do with the previous step of 'תוס).&lrm;[^2]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">&lrm;הוה אמינא of 'תוס (how did he understand our 'גמ before his answer and what changed. If 'תוס brings a different 'גמ in his answer, how did he understand that 'גמ when he asked his question).[^2]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Multiple answers in 'תוס (each answer has weaknesses. What are they? Sometimes, each answer becomes the basis of a הלכה למעשה מחלוקת, so getting clarity is important).[^2]</span>
#### Others Who Agree
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The חזון איש,&lrm;[^4] the ספר דרכי הגמרא&lrm;,[^5] and the רמח"ל[^6] talk about the tremendous importance of focus on the words of the חז"ל. </span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The חזון איש further says that true ameilus is pushing to understand the words of חז"ל to the point where it is difficult. This opens a שער אורה and one is זוכה to all of the segulos of the תורה. One needs to be cautious about relying too much on סברא.&lrm;[^7]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Dovid learned to focus on the הוה אמינא of 'תוס from the חפץ חיים.&lrm;[^2]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rabbi Akiva Eiger viewed 'תוס's הוה אמינא as 'תוס's פשיט פשט in Shas.[^8]</span>
#### Balance of סברא and לשון
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At the same time, this has to be balanced with סברא.&lrm;[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Sometimes, a ראשון may want to reference a few different possible interpretations of the 'גמ in his answer. As such, no single interpretation reads smoothly and it is not supposed to. In this situation, saying a pshat that fits with the לשון is not going to be correct. This is a limitation on using לשון.&lrm;[^9]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Obviously, סברא and bekiyos are important. The point is that these should not form the main basis of one's pshat.[^10]</span>
### Corollaries
#### Focus on ראשונים
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">For this reason, Rav Dovid would focus on ראשונים as they were closer to the emes and so there is more to pull out.[^10] (And they wrote more exactly so one can rely on diyukim&lrm;[^11])</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Chaim uncovered many of the yesodos developed in Rav חיים על HaRambam from רש"י and 'תוס.&lrm;[^12] Rav Chaim said that the goal is to uncover the chiddushim of the ראשונים, not come up with our own.[^13]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Practically, we do need help from the אחרונים to be able to understand the ראשונים (Rav Dovid encouraged his תלמידים to focus on the major אחרונים and even gave a specific סדר to do this), but the main focus should still be on understanding the ראשונים.&lrm;[^14]</span>
##### "Defense" Of אחרונים
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This certainly does not mean that the אחרונים should be treated with anything less than the utmost respect.</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There were tremendous גדולים who spent a great deal of effort on אחרונים. Rav Boruch Ber in Birchas Shmuel deals with and defends the words of the gedolei אחרונים. Similarly, Rav Chaim Leib Tiktinsky (of the Mir. See [[Jewish History in Lithuania and Belarus#Mir#Continuation of the Yeshiva|here]]) would often refer to the פני יהושע in his shiurim.[^15]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Dovid one time walked by תלמידים learning a Rav Chaim. These תלמידים had a question on Rav Chaim and quickly dismissed him. Rav Dovid was very pained by this. Rav Chaim was a tremendous genius and he would work so hard, he would nearly die from toil.[^16]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It may be difficult to learn אחרונים because they are more verbose and so there is less room to say one's own תורה.</span>
   One combats this by thinking about how these גדולים had tremendous intelligence, they would only write after tremendous עיון, what they wrote was based on their wealth of knowledge they had accumulated, and they had tremendous yiras shamayim and siyata dishmaya.[^17]
   Additionally, Rav Dovid would advice people to read the introduction of a ספר where they talk about the maalos of the author of the ספר before learning it to give one the proper respect for the author.[^16]
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There are numerous benefits to learning אחרונים, such as:</span>
	1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Many times (if not nearly all of the time) the אחרון will propose a פשט that the person would not have thought of on his own. (One can still "take credit" for working out what the אחרון is saying, particularly if they were able to understand more than what initially meets the eye from learning him.)[^18]</span>
	2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Even if the person did think of the פשט on their own, one can "hide behind the אחרון" to be able to say such a חידוש.</span>
	3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If one has a פשט but has questions from bekiyos, if they see the פשט in an אחרון, they can be confident that there are answers to the question and that this is a viable מהלך. In addition, this galvanizes a person to look for the answer (as they know that there is one).</span>
#### Focus on Major מחלוקתים
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Many ראשונים will talk about all the major points of the sugya. This gives one a lot of material to work with.[^12]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Also, this pushes one to figure out why the other מפרשים disagree. (It should be a small point of contention, not a wide מחלוקת.)&lrm;[^12]</span>
#### Answering Questions
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One develops answers not by "giving answers", but rather by getting a clear understanding of the sugya to the point that one sees that the question isn't a question (similar to Rav Chaim Brisker's מהלך)&lrm;[^19]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One can answer questions using bekiyos but it is usually farther from the truth.[^12]</span>
## Hard Work
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One needs to exert themselves to understand pshat, even an iluy.[^20] In fact, it may be harder for an iluy to work hard and not rely on their initial take.[^21]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One can read a lot of nice pshatim in a sugya. But one won't really understand them without putting in effort.[^22]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One needs to put in utmost effort. This is what real התמדה is; it's not how long one learns, its how intensely one focuses when learning.[^23]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One can arguably be עובר on בטל תורה if one learns with a lack effort.[^24]</span>
### How Far One Should Go
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is extremely important to create chaburos on sugyos (work deeply on a sugya to try and understand pshat through one's own own take) to really get to get one to work hard on learning.[^25]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A person's goal should be to push themselves to the point of "עד מקום שידו מגעת", whether they are working on a chabura, or trying to understand their rebbi's שיעור.&lrm;[^26]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At the same time, one can always be motivated to push themselves harder, even צדיקים. There is no limit to how far one can push themselves.[^27] We see this by the דור דעה that they were able to push themselves harder to understand משה's תורה when משה was about to die.[^28] We also see that דוד was able to push himself harder to find a solution when כלל ישראל was dying because they had not honored Shaul properly when he died.[^29]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Even so, one should set some sort of time limits for how long they spend on a sugya.[^30]</span>
### Hard Work in Tangential Areas
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Putting effort into areas that are tangential to the learning also helps one with the learning.[^31]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Avimi traveled to Rav Yochanan, his תלמיד, to help him work out a הלכה he forgot. Because of the effort of going there, and because Avimi honored the תורה by being willing to travel to his תלמיד, they were able to figure it out.[^32]</span>
### Benefits
#### Help Fighting One's יצר
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Learning תורה בעיון and with effort can help one overcome his יצר (see [[#^8c01d0|here]], [[#^a896ee|here]], and [[#^b72bbb|here]]).</span>
#### Attachment to תורה
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Working hard on תורה increases one's attachment to תורה.&lrm;[^33]</span>
#### People Listen to You
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Learning תורה causes people to listen to you.[^34]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The מדרש says that Hashem told משה that כלל ישראל would listen to him because of his ameilus in תורה.&lrm;[^35] This was the reason that was highlighted, more so than all of משה's other maalos.[^34]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This may work through some sort of spiritual type of force, or it could be that כלל ישראל appreciated משה's hard work in trying to understand the תורה to be able to give it over to them.[^34] {<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">According to this, I don't see why that would apply to other people unless they are also teaching תורה to the people listening.</span>}</span>
### Discussed by חז"ל and גדולים
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The Maharzu says that if one doesn't try hard, he won't be able to grasp תורה.&lrm;[^36] (He might imply that one may achieve minimal understand, at best.[^11])</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to רבינו בחיי, משה was זוכה to his greatness because he put in tremendous effort to be "מעיין בחכמה".&lrm;[^37]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to the יפה תואר, Betzalel was able to be mechavein to the דעת of Hashem through his hard work in תורה.&lrm;[^38]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This seems to be what חז"ל mean by לא יגעתי ומצאתי אל תאמן. One needs to put in work to be able to understand.[^22]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The חתם סופר talks about how even the greatest chachomim need to put in tremendous work.[^39]</span>
6. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Naftali Trop said that he had a a difficult time pushing himself because he was very quick and was able to quickly get a working pshat. However, he pushed himself to work hard anyway. He said that that was why he became who he became.[^21]</span>
7. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">When Rav Naftali Trop would prepare for שיעור, he would focus so hard that he would become oblivious to the world.[^40] In a letter to his sister, Rav Naftali wrote that he would focus so hard when preparing for שיעור that he would become sick.[^41]</span>
8. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The חובות הלבבות says that learning תורה strengthens one's sechel and helps them see through the yetzer.[^42] It seems reasonable that the more one utilizes their sechel when learning, the more effective this is.[^43]</span> ^8c01d0
9. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav ישראל says that one should learn all of the הלכות of in inyan with tremendous עיון to help grow in that area.[^44]</span> ^a896ee
10. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The חזון איש says that the more בעיון one learns, the more one appreciates his mission in this world, despises תאות היצר, and desires חכמה.&lrm;[^45]</span> ^b72bbb
11. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The רמח"ל says that the main hashpa from Hashem is through a connection to תורה, which is achieved through learning תורה בעיון.&lrm;[^46]</span>
12. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In many instances, חז"ל were purposely vague so that people would have to work hard on the חז"ל and be able to receive the benefits of that.[^47]</span>
13. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Yitzchak Elchanan Spektor (the גדול הדור of his time) was מתיר many difficult cases of agunos. Rav Yitzchak Elchanan did not feel that he was particularly brighter than many of the other גדולים. However, he felt he cared more about the agunos, and that spurred him to work harder.[^48]</span>
14. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">See [[#^da3f84|here]], [[#^ae8f5d|here]], and [[#^10a174|here]].</span>
## Bekiyos
### Purpose
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Bekiyos is important not just to know תורה, but because some topics in the 'גמ are broken up and discussed in multiple places. Without seeing all of the relevant gemaros, it won't be possible to come to an understanding of that sugya.[^49]</span>
### Key Points to Learning Bekiyos
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is important to combine learn bekiyos with some level of עיון because:</span>
	1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This helps one gain an understand of the inyanim one is learning with enough depth that one can apply what one learned to their actual עיון seder.[^30]</span>
	2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One is able to uncover inyanim that are really being discussed in one's 'גמ but are hard to see at first glance.[^50]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As such, one should learn 'תוס with basic מפרשים, particularly the Maharasha, and the רא"ש. In addition, one should try to learn additional material about the topic that is being discussed in one's bekiyos seder.[^30]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The Marasha in particular is extremely deep and trains one to think hard to understand it. גדולים from past generations, such as Rabbi Akiva Eiger, were extremely machshiv the Maharasha.[^51] {<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Not sure if this should go here.</span>}</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One should spend most of their time learning bekiyos.[^30]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One should learn 2-3 daf per week, 2-3 times. The first time should be relatively quick, the second time a person should go slower and be more miayein, and the third time a person should focus on remaining questions.[^30]</span>
## Chazara
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Chazara is extremely important.[^52]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One should even chazar past masechtos one did.[^52]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One should chazar what they have done numerous times.[^52]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A practical strategy is to review 3 blatt in a row, then move up one blatt and review 3 blatt in a row, and keep doing that, this way one reviews the same blatt multiple times. For example, one should review daf ב, ג, ד, and then ג, ד, ה, and then ד, ה, ו, etc.[^52]</span>
# Teaching
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There are many factors that a rebbi needs to balance when teaching. It takes tremendous wisdom and forethought to be able to try and get it right.[^53]</span>
## Making Talmidim Work
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A rebbi should give over his שיעור in a way that forces his talmidim to work to understand because of the important gains one gets from [[#Hard Work|working hard when learning]].[^54]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At the same time, the rebbi has to make sure that he שיעור is not too difficult to understand.[^53]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As such, the rebbi needs to have a good grasp of the strength of his talmidim.[^53]</span>
### Potential Pitfall
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A rebbi can't focus on his own growth. The main point by far is the growth of the talmidim and doing what is best for them.</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The 'גמ&lrm;[^55] says that a rebbi needs to be like a malach. The simple understanding is that the rebbi needs to work on himself to that point. However, the ספר המקנה&lrm;[^56] learns pshat that the same way that a malach is an "עומד" (it doesn't grow), a rebbi can't care about his own growth. He has to view himself like a "עומד", just like a malach.</span>
## Review Time
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The rebbi has to make sure that there is time for the talmidim to review the material.[^53]</span>
# Giving Chaburos
## Work Hard
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As mentioned [[#How Far One Should Go|earlier]], one should always work to the point of "עד מקום שידו מגעת".</span>
## Working on the Same Sugya
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There can be an advantage to working on the same sugya multiple times, meaning, that even if a person has already said a chabura on a sugya, if there are more pockets, there is an advantage to try and say a chabura on that sugya rather than a different one. This is because the person has already set up the sugya and put in the work to understand the background information. Going to a new sugya would require the person to have to rebuild the sugya from scratch.[^57]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One just has to be careful that they are not simply repackaging what they said previously and just saying it again.[^57]</span>
# Stories and Sayings About Rav Dovid
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Yaakov Kaminetzky was tremendously machshiv Rab Dovid. Rav Yaakov felt that Rav Dovid was uniquely talented at getting pshat.[^2] He sent his son (Rav Binyomin Kaminetzky) to learn by Rav Dovid and said that he would have sent Rav Binyomin to America to learn by Rav Dovid even if he still lived in Europe (meaning, over the many great roshei yeshiva of Europe in that time).[^19]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Yaakov learned with Rav Dovid when Rav Yaakov first came to Slabodka and said that the two זמנים they spent together formed the basis of Rav Yaakov's learning.[^19]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Yaakov described himself as a תלמיד-chaver of Rav Dovid.[^19]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If Rav Dovid would not be mechavein to 'תוס's question on רש"י, on the Maharasha's question on Tosofas when learning with the חפץ חיים, the חפץ חיים would tell Rav Dovid that he was not focusing properly (when the questions were from within the sugya, not from bekiyos).[^40]</span> ^da3f84
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Dovid would sometimes focus on the same question for hours without moving until he got an answer or got a headache.[^58]</span> ^ae8f5d
6. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Dovid would focus so hard that he would start sweating, even when he was learning in the winter at home (and he couldn't afford heat).[^59]</span> ^10a174
7. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Dovid had a tremendous middah of emes and so it was very hard to get a הסכמה out of him for a chabura.[^60] (Presumably because every chabura will have some weak points.)</span>
   A תלמיד decided to try and answer all of the questions that רע"א ended off with צריך עיון. Rav Dovid convinced him not to do this because he was concerned that, in his effort to accomplish his goal, the תלמיד may accept answers that were really not correct.[^61]
