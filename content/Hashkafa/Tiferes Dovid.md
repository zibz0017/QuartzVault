# דרך הלימוד
## Overarching Goal
1. The main point is for one to put all of his efforts into trying to understand pshat in the rishonim and try to tease out everything that the rishonim are saying.[^1]
2. Saying "big chiddushim" or "geshmake Torah" is definitely not the goal.[^1]
3. There is a particular focus on trying to understand Tosafos.[^1]
## General Points
### Extracting from Chazal
#### Main Idea
1. There is more written "between the lines" than what is actually "on the line".[^2]
2. One develops his understanding of the sugya through intense focus on extracting from what is written, rather than coming up with one's own svaros. The advantage of this is that one's Torah is built on chazal, rather than oneself.[^3]
#### Particular Points to Examine
1. Lashon.[^2]
2. "כלומר"s in Rashi (Rashi isn't saying the pashut pshat. What is the pashut pshat and why didn't Rashi like it?)[^2]
3. <span style="visibility:hidden">l</span>המשך in Tosafos (what does a question have to do with the previous step of Tosafos).[^2]
4. <span style="visibility:hidden">l</span>הוה אמינא of Tosafos (how did he understand our gemara before his answer and what changed. If Tosafos brings a different gemara in his answer, how did he understand that gemara when he asked his question).[^2]
5. Multiple answers in Tosafos (each answer has weaknesses. What are they? Sometimes, each answer becomes the basis of a halacha lemaysa machlokes, so getting clarity is important).[^2]
#### Others who Agree
1. The Chazon Ish,[^4] the sefer דרכי הגמרא<span style="visibility:hidden">l</span>,[^5] and the Ramchal[^6] talk about the tremendous importance of focus on the words of the chazal. 
2. The Chazon Ish further says that true ameilus is pushing to understand the words of chazal to the point where it is difficult. This opens a שער אורה and one is zocheh to all of the segulos of the Torah. One needs to be cautious about relying too much on svara.[^7]
3. Rav Dovid learned to focus on the הוה אמינא of Tosafos from the Chofetz Chaim.[^2]
4. Rabbi Akiva Eiger viewed Tosafos's הוה אמינא as Tosafos's פשיט פשט in Shas.[^8]
#### Balance of Svara and Lashon
1. At the same time, this has to be balanced with svara.[^1]
2. Sometimes, a rishon may want to reference a few different possible interpretations of the gemara in his answer. As such, no single interpretation reads smoothly and it is not supposed to. In this situation, saying a pshat that fits with the lashon is not going to be correct. This is a limitation on using lashon.[^9]
3. Obviously, svara and bekiyos are important. The point is that these should not form the main basis of one's pshat.[^10]
### Corollaries
#### Focus on Rishonim
1. For this reason, Rav Dovid would focus on rishonim as they were closer to the emes and so there is more to pull out.[^10] (And they wrote more exactly so one can rely on diyukim[^11])
2. Rav Cahim uncovered many of the yesodos developed in Rav Chaim Al HaRambam from Rashi and Tosafos.[^12] Rav Chaim said that the goal is to uncover the chiddushim of the rishonim, not come up with our own.[^13]
#### Focus on Major Machlokesim
1. Many rishonim will talk about all the major points of the sugya. This gives one a lot of material to work with.[^12]
2. Also, this pushes one to figure out why the other mefarshim disagree. (It should be a small point of contention, not a wide machlokes.)[^12]
#### Answering Questions
1. One develops answers not by "giving answers", but rather by getting a clear understanding of the sugya to the point that one sees that the question isn't a question (similar to Rav Chaim Brisker's mehalech)[^14]
2. One can answer questions using bekiyos but it is usually farther from the truth.[^12]
## Hard Work
1. One needs to exert themselves to understand pshat, even an iluy.[^15] In fact, it may be harder for an iluy to work hard and not rely on their initial take.[^16]
2. One can read a lot of nice pshatim in a sugya. But one won't really understand them without putting in effort.[^17]
3. One needs to put in utmost effort. This is what real התמדה is; it's not how long one learns, its how intensely one focuses when learning.[^18]
4. One can arguably be ovver on bittul Torah if one learns with a lack effort.[^19]
### How Far One Should Go
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is extremely important to create chaburos on sugyos (work deeply on a sugya to try and understand pshat through one's own own take) to really get to get one to work hard on learning.[^20]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A person's goal should be to push themselves to the point of "עד מקום שידו מגעת", whether they are working on a chabura, or trying to understand their rebbi's shiur.[^21]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At the same time, one can always be motivated to push themselves harder, even tzadikim. There is no limit to how far one can push themselves.[^22] We see this by the דור דעה that they were able to push themselves harder to understand Moshe's Torah when Moshe was about to die.[^23] We also see that Dovid was able to push himself harder to find a solution when Klal Yisroel was dying because they had not honored Shaul properly when he died.[^24]</span>
4. Even so, one should set some sort of time limits for how long they spend on a sugya.[^25]
### Hard Work in Tangential Areas
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Putting effort into areas that are tangential to the learning also helps one with the learning.[^26]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Avimi traveled to Rav Yochanan, his talmid, to help him work out a halacha he forgot. Because of the effort of going there, and because Avimi honored the Torah by being willing to travel to his talmid, they were able to figure it out.[^27]</span>
### Benefits
#### Help Fighting One's יצר
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Learning Torah בעיון and with effort can help one overcome his יצר (see [[#^8c01d0|here]], [[#^a896ee|here]], and [[#^b72bbb|here]]).</span>
#### Attachment to Torah
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Working hard on Torah increases one's attachment to Torah.[^28]</span>
#### People Listen to You
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Learning Torah causes people to listen to you.[^29]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The Medrash says that Hashem told Moshe that Klal Yisroel would listen to him because of his ameilus in Torah.[^30] This was the reason that was highlighted, more so than all of Moshe's other maalos.[^29]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This may work through some sort of spiritual type of force, or it could be that Klal Yisroel appreciated Moshe's hard work in trying to understand the torah to be able to give it over to them.[^29] {<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">According to this, I don't see why that would apply to other people unless they are also teaching Torah to the people listening.</span>}</span>
### Discussed by Chazal and Gedolim
1. The Maharzu says that if one doesn't try hard, he won't be able to grasp Torah.[^31] (He might imply that one may achieve minimal understand, at best.[^11])
2. According to Rabbeinu Bechayei, Moshe was zoche to his greatness because he put in tremendous effort to be "מעיין בחכמה".<span style="visibility:hidden">l</span>[^32]
3. According to the יפה תואר, Betzalel was able to be mechavein to the daas of Hashem through his hard work in Torah.[^33]
4. This seems to be what Chazal mean by לא יגעתי ומצאתי אל תאמן. One needs to put in work to be able to understand.[^17]
5. The Chasam Sofer talks about how even the greatest chachomim need to put in tremendous work.[^34]
6. Rav Naftali Trop said that he had a a difficult time pushing himself because he was very quick and was able to quickly get a working pshat. However, he pushed himself to work hard anyway. He said that that was why he became who he became.[^16]
7. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">When Rav Naftali Trop would prepare for shiur, he would focus so hard that he would become oblivious to the world.[^35] In a letter to his sister, Rav Naftali wrote that he would focus so hard when preparing for shiur that he would become sick.[^36]</span>
8. The חובות הלבבות says that learning Torah strengthens one's sechel and helps them see through the yetzer.[^37] It seems reasonable that the more one utilizes their sechel when learning, the more effective this is.[^38] ^8c01d0
9. Rav Yisroel says that one should learn all of the halachos of in inyan with tremendous iyun to help grow in that area.[^39] ^a896ee
10. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The Chazon Ish says that the more בעיון one learns, the more one appreciates his mission in this world, despises תאות היצר, and desires חכמה.<span style="visibility:hidden">l</span>[^40]</span> ^b72bbb
11. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The Ramchal says that the main hashpa from Hashem is through a connection to Torah, which is achieved through learning Torah בעיון.<span style="visibility:hidden">l</span>[^41]</span>
12. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In many instances, Chazal were purposely vague so that people would have to work hard on the chazal and be able to receive the benefits of that.[^42]</span>
13. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Yitzchak Elchanan Spektor (the gadol hador of his time) was matir many difficult cases of agunos. Rav Yitzchak Elchanan did not feel that he was particularly brighter than many of the other gedolim. However, he felt he cared more about the agunos, and that spurred him to work harder.[^43]</span>

## Bekiyos
### Purpose
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Bekiyos is important not just to know Torah, but because some topics in the gemara are broken up and discussed in multiple places. Without seeing all of the relevant gemaros, it won't be possible to come to an understanding of that sugya.[^44]</span>
### Key Points to Learning Bekiyos
1. It is important to combine learn bekiyos with some level of iyun because:
	1. This helps one gain an understand of the inyanim one is learning with enough depth that one can apply what one learned to their actual iyun seder.[^25]
	2. One is able to uncover inyanim that are really being discussed in one's gemara but are hard to see at first glance.[^45]
2. As such, one should learn Tosafos with basic mefarshim, particularly the Maharasha, and the Rosh. In addition, one should try to learn additional material about the topic that is being discussed in one's bekiyos seder.[^25]
3. The Marasha in particular is extremely deep and trains one to think hard to understand it. Gedolim from past generations, such as Rabbi Akiva Eiger, were extremely machshiv the Maharasha.[^46] {<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Not sure if this should go here.</span>}
4. One should spend most of their time learning bekiyos.[^25]
5. One should learn 2-3 daf per week, 2-3 times. The first time should be relatively quick, the second time a person should go slower and be more miayein, and the third time a person should focus on remaining questions.[^25]
## Chazara
1. Chazara is extremely important.[^47]
2. One should even chazar past masechtos one did.[^47]
3. One should chazar what they have done numerous times.[^47]
4. A practical strategy is to review 3 blatt in a row, then move up one blatt and review 3 blatt in a row, and keep doing that, this way one reviews the same blatt multiple times. For example, one should review daf ב, ג, ד, and then ג, ד, ה, and then ד, ה, ו, etc.[^47]
# Teaching
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There are many factors that a rebbi needs to balance when teaching. It takes tremendous wisdom and forethought to be able to try and get it right.[^48]</span>
## Making Talmidim Work
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A rebbi should give over his shiur in a way that forces his talmidim to work to understand because of the important gains one gets from [[#Hard Work|working hard when learning]].[^49]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At the same time, the rebbi has to make sure that he shiur is not too difficult to understand.[^48]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As such, the rebbi needs to have a good grasp of the strength of his talmidim.[^48]</span>
### Potential Pitfall
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A rebbi can't focus on his own growth. The main point by far is the growth of the talmidim and doing what is best for them.</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The gemara[^50] says that a rebbi needs to be like a malach. The simple understanding is that the rebbi needs to work on himself to that point. However, the ספר המקנה<span style="visibility:hidden">l</span>[^51] learns pshat that the same way that a malach is an "עומד" (it doesn't grow), a rebbi can't care about his own growth. He has to view himself like a "עומד", just like a malach.</span>
## Review Time
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The rebbi has to make sure that there is time for the talmidim to review the material.[^48]</span>
# Giving Chaburos
## Work Hard
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As mentioned [[#How Far One Should Go|earlier]], one should always work to the point of "עד מקום שידו מגעת".</span>
## Working on the Same Sugya
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There can be an advantage to working on the same sugya multiple times, meaning, that even if a person has already said a chabura on a sugya, if there are more pockets, there is an advantage to try and say a chabura on that sugya rather than a different one. This is because the person has already set up the sugya and put in the work to understand the background information. Going to a new sugya would require the person to have to rebuild the sugya from scratch.[^52]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One just has to be careful that they are not simply repackaging what they said previously and just saying it again.[^52]</span>
# Stories and Sayings About Rav Dovid
1. Rav Yaakov Kaminetzky was tremendously machshiv Rab Dovid. Rav Yaakov felt that Rav Dovid was uniquely talented at getting pshat.[^2] He sent his son (Rav Binyomin Kaminetzky) to learn by Rav Dovid and said that he would have sent Rav Binyomin to America to learn by Rav Dovid even if he still lived in Europe (meaning, over the many great roshei yeshiva of Europe in that time).[^14]
2. Rav Yaakov learned with Rav Dovid when Rav Yaakov first came to Slabodka and said that the two zmanim they spent together formed the basis of Rav Yaakov's learning.[^14]
3. Rav Yaakov described himself as a talmid-chaver of Rav Dovid.[^14]
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If Rav Dovid would not be mechavein to Tosafos's question on Rashi, on the Maharasha's question on Tosofas when learning with the Chofetz Chaim, the Chofetz Chaim would tell Rav Dovid that he was not focusing properly (when the questions were from within the sugya, not from bekiyos).[^35]</span> ^da3f84
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Dovid would sometimes focus on the same question for hours without moving until he got an answer or got a headache.[^53]</span> ^ae8f5d
6. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rav Dovid would focus so hard that he would start sweating, even when he was learning in the winter at home (and he couldn't afford heat).[^54]</span> ^10a174
