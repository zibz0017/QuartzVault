# Basics
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Investors invest money into a VC firm (also called a fund).[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The fund looks for companies that it believes will grow and invests the money with those companies.[^1]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The fund does the research into the companies while the investors supply the money.[^1]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The fund gets a percentage of ownership in the company (called equity).[^1]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In addition to the money, the fund sometimes offers advice to the company they invested in.[^1]</span>
6. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The manager of the VC firm is called a general partner (GP) and the investors are called limited partners (LP).[^1]</span>
# Fees
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">LPs generally pay a fee of about 2% of the total fund value.</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In addition the GP generally takes 20% of the profit (called carried interest, or just "carry").[^1]</span>
# Risk
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">These investments are high risk.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Around 75% of the companies fail.[^1]</span>
# History
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The first VC firm started in 1946, called American Research and Development Corporation (ARDC).[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There were individual investors before this (like the Rockefellers).[^1]</span>
# Impact
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">VC has been involved in many major companies, like Airbnb, Google, and OpenAI.[^1]</span>
