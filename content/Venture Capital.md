# Basics
1. Investors invest money into a VC firm (also called a fund).[^1]
2. The fund looks for companies that it believes will grow and invests the money with those companies.[^1]
3. The fund does the research into the companies while the investors supply the money.[^1]
4. The fund gets a percentage of ownership in the company (called equity).[^1]
5. In addition to the money, the fund sometimes offers advice to the company they invested in.[^1]
6. The manager of the VC firm is called a general partner (GP) and the investors are called limited partners (LP).[^1]
# Fees
1. LPs generally pay a fee of about 2% of the total fund value.
2. In addition the GP generally takes 20% of the profit (called carried interest, or just "carry").[^1]
# Risk
1. These investments are high risk.[^1]
2. Around 75% of the companies fail.[^1]
# History
1. The first VC firm started in 1946, called American Research and Development Corporation (ARDC).[^1]
2. There were individual investors before this (like the Rockefellers).[^1]
# Impact
1. VC has been involved in many major companies, like Airbnb, Google, and OpenAI.[^1]
