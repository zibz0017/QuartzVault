# Description
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">A leveraged ETF tracks either an asset or an index and moves some multiple of the percentage change of that asset or index. For example, if you have a 2x leveraged ETF that tracks the S&P, if the S&P goes up 1%, this ETF will go up 2%.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It increases risk and reward compared to standard ETFs.[^1]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This multiplication is accomplished by using derivatives and debt.[^1]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Usually, these ETFs are 2x or 3x.[^1]</span>
# Reverse Leveraged ETFs
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Some leveraged ETFs have a negative multiplier (-2x or -3x).</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">That means that if the tracked asset or index decreases by 1%, this ETF grows by 2 or 3 percent.</span>
