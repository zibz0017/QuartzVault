# Unorganized Notes
- IFS paradigm: We are made of "multiple parts". (Will be elaborated.)[^1]
- Usual paradigm: We need to control/push through our bad impulses.[^1]
- History of sources for this form of thinking such as:[^1]
	- Religion.
	- Freudian psychology.
	- Various philosophers (Machiavelli, Hobbes, Nietzsche, and many others).
- Shaming ("failure if you mess up")/"drill seargenting" yourself for bad impulses causes them to "fight back". Either ineffective, or makes oneself super uptight.[^1]
- IFS doesn't dismiss willpower. Difference is Self loves and nurtures parts that cause these impulses.[^1]
- Mindfulness is somewhat similar to IFS, involves acceptance of emotions.[^1]
- The parts that are doing "negative" things are doing them to protect the other parts which are vulnerable or to protect the Self.[^1]
- Even most destructive parts have positive intentions.[^1]
- Parts are often frozen in past traumas where their protection was needed.[^1]
- Parts are extremely useful once they get out of their extreme protection.[^1]
- Parts carry burdens. These are beliefs and emotions.[^2]
- These can come from personal experiences and traumas (personal burdens). They can also come from family history, culture, or ethnic group one is part of (legacy burdens). This second group can be harder to pick up on.[^2]
- These burdens teach one what to expect from the world.[^2]
- Parts are not their burdens. We want to heal the part from their burden.[^2]
- First one might acknowledge the help the part is trying to give (for example, an addict might take drugs to avoid the pain of being a failure. The urge to take drugs is a part trying to protect the person from the pain). Then we help unburden the part (I assume this will be talked about later).[^2]
- Parts then take on new useful roles.[^2]
- Even the worst criminals and murderers have no bad parts. Their drive to do what they did stems from a part trying to help. (The author personally successfully used IFS on terrible criminals, which was helpful for rehabilitation.)[^2]
- Therapist helps clients form a better relationship with his/her parts.[^2]
- The client will speak for different parts. Sometimes, other parts want to interject. Asking the other parts to "step back" to allow a current part to speak is helpful. This also allows the Self to come through (will be discussed later).[^2]
- Burdened parts can be coxed to reveal their history, and the client can show the part compassion.[^2]
- There is a part of the "self", "who I really am", "my core", etc. The Self can't be corrupted. It knows how to heal oneself.[^2]
- You can feel your own parts. Find a calm place and feel internally. Feel for emotions or feelings (called trailheads). See how you feel about those. Try to get yourself to feel openness and curiosity about it.[^3]
- You will feel this somewhere in your body. You can ask it questions and it may answer. Don't think of answers. The answers will come to you.[^3]
- Show love and appreciation for the part and when you are done, assure the part that there will be another time for it to speak.[^3]
- Usually, the parts blend into the Self. This mindfulness allows one to unblend the part and look at it.[^3]
- If you feel anything other than curiosity or openness about the part, it means that your Self is still blended with other parts.[^3]
- Parts appreciate it when you give them attention.[^3]
- Focusing on one part can allow one to grab onto multiple parts that are part of the same cluster.[^3]
- Drawing a part (meaning, with a paper and pen, or whatever), and looking at the drawing afterwards helps one focus on it. The drawing can be anything. Whatever you think.[^3]
- You can pick up on how the parts interact. Some may protect others. Other may be fighting.[^3]
- A part may blend with the Self and take over. It will make you feel and get you to act in a certain way. The part is trying to protect the Self because it thinks the Self can't and something horrible will happen if they don't step in. This may have been taught by a past trauma.[^4]
- Despite their best efforts, parts aren't very good at this. They will cause a variety of unhealthy behaviors (overreacting, taking care of others and neglecting oneself, etc.)[^4]
- Some people are blended so often, they don't even realize it.[^4]
- Some parts are triggered by specific events.[^4]
- The Self desires to connect to others, and can see through the actions of others to their underlying pains. Blended parts cover this up.[^4]
- Important to convince the parts that it is safe to unblend. Noticing parts helps with that. This also helps you care about the parts. The Self naturally cares about the parts. It just needs to be uncovered.[^4]
- Parts sometimes think that you are much younger than you are. Ask them how old they think you are, and then tell them the truth. They may be surprised.[^5]
- It's recommended to have daily IFS meditations. Check in with the parts that you are working on, and then invite other parts to come forward if they want.[^5]
- This may lead to more of a feeling of inner peace and sometimes, a vibrating energy. This is similar to other forms of meditation.[^5]
- The more you do this, the more you will begin to realize throughout your day when you are feeling stressed or other things like that. There may be physical tells as well (tightened mouth, hunched shoulders, etc.) This is because a part is blending with the self. You will naturally get better and reassuring the part that the Self has it under control and it will give the Self space to do that.[^5]
- The goal of IFS is to liberate these parts from these rolls and to put regain trust and leadership in the Self. You need to reassure the parts that the Self has it under control.[^5]
- Parts and people and not inherently flawed or destructive.[^6]
- Parts are useful. Once they are released from the roles they are forced into, you can access those benefits.[^6]
- Parts are trying to keep you and other parts safe.[^6]
- Parts are unique from each other. They have different personalities, talents, and desires. They are basically their own unique people. The "angry part" isn't just angry, the same way an "angry person" isn't just angry. It has a whole range of personality.[^6]
- These parts feel like they have to protect you alone. Talking to them let's them know that they are not.[^6]
- Parts are innate in every person. Even babies have them (although many are dormant, or develop later).[^6]
- Parts provide qualities (like joy and sensitivity), and resources (the ability to focus and problem-solving).[^6]
- Parts (which are burdened) can hurt you in many ways. Constantly berate you, push you to take care of others to an extreme, cause nightmares, psychosomatic disorders, and others (many are what are listed in the DSM).[^6]
- A therapist can get the patient to be in touch with a part by picturing a themselves in a particular episode that they want to work through, and try to find that "you" inside yourself. Like if someone was bullied in high school, try to find that bullied highschooler in you. Then, engage with him.
- Sometimes, a person may feel multiple emotions from one event (fear, anxiety, and anger). The person may have to work on all of them.
