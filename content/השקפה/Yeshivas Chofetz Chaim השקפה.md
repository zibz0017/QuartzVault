## Importance of Saying Chaburos
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Saying Chaburos is really important for one's growth.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Saying a chabura forces one to push himself to the full extent of his abilities. The more one does this, the more they grow.[^1]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In addition, saying chaburos give a certain pressure to have things clear, because if you do not, the people you are saying the chabura over to will tear into you.[^1]</span>
## Pilpul Shiurim
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Pilpul shiurim are very difficult. The growth comes from the difficulty.[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Even if one "drops out" before the שיעור is over, the growth one gets from working and struggling to get whatever one was able to get from the pilpul שיעור makes it worth it to have them, despite all of the early dropping out.[^2]</span>
### General Advice
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Speak to your fellow talmidim and ask them your questions and to explain steps. Don't just try to only get things from the rebbi.[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As mentioned before, one should keep in mind the fact that the growth comes from the struggle.[^2]</span>

# גשמיות
## Setting Limits
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">One needs to have limits on גשמיות. This is because gahmius is the antithesis of תורה and causes the תורה not to have an affect on a person. These is the famous shmuz of Shevna Hasofer who was a tremendous תלמיד חכם, but because of his attachment to גשמיות, he ended up going off of the דרך, and the reason for this was because the attachment to גשמיות did not allow the תורה to penetrate and change him.[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Once someone gets used to a certain level of גשמיות it is difficult to give that up and "go down" a level. As such, increasing one's level of gashius is not some short-term commitment. It may make a difference down the road.[^2]</span>
## Wealth Vs גשמיות
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">To some degree, the wealthier one gets, the more גשמיות they can have. However, one still needs to have limits because of the reasons discussed above.[^2]</span>
# Government Programs
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There is a negative to being a taker and taking government programs.[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">That being said, there is a balance that needs to be made. If one is going to have to leave ישיבה, or if one will stay but there will be financial stress, or if a mother is going to have to work a second job and have less time for her kids, it would seem reasonable to accept government programs or to take a pay cut to be able to qualify. One should keep in mind that this is not ideal, though.[^2] (I assume that  this does not mean that if someone feels depressed or something about taking government programs, they should keep this in mind. ## Ideal חינוך Positions
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There is a misconception that some positions in הרבצת התורה (like being a בית מדרש rebbi), is more ideal than other positions (like being an elementary school rebbi or being a rav).[^5]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This is false. There is no such thing as an objectively ideal position. The "ideal position" is different for every person, and it is entirely different for every individual person. There is simply no way to know which position has the largest impact.[^5]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Rabbi Grunblatt personally had a rebbi in 2nd or 3rd grade who he felt had a major impact on his life.[^5]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In many communities, regular people can have impacts on people that the rov is not in position to have.[^5]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Regardless of whether one is in an official חינוך position or not, one will always be משפיע on people on so one has to do their best to make sure that they are impacting people in a positive way.[^5]</span>

# White Shirts
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The Rosh ישיבה felt that people should wear white shirts because of כבוד הבריות. However the Rosh ישיבה was concerned that wearing white shirts could lead to גאווה. As such, the ישיבה didn't push it.[^9]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The ישיבה world has shifted toward wearing white shirts. Rabbi Harris felt that this wasn't due to feelings of כבוד הבריות, but rather because people simply don't want to be weird. (To me it sounded like rebbi was saying that if one feels the כבוד הבריות, one should wear a white shirt, but most people aren't holding there).[^9]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This doesn't mean that one shouldn't wear white shirts if one isn't doing it because of כבוד הבריות. There certainly is a סברא not to stand out and be weird.[^9]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If we would realize that everyone around us is just a prop and it is only us and Hashem, we wouldn't feel embarrassed. However, it is certainly not the norm for people to so fully דהר this and it is perfectly fine to feel uncomfortable for being so different.[^9]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Additionally, it sounded like rebbi thought that the גאווה סברא wasn't such a strong סברא, especially בזמן הזה that everyone wears white shirts.[^9]</span>
6. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Guys suggested that the white shirts had come from chassidim and had "infiltrated" the litvishe world. Rabbi Harris disagreed. Rebbi said that when he was growing up, the non-Jewish workers (in white-collared jobs) used to wear white shirts.[^9]</span>
7. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">(On a side note, there has been a tremendous ירידת הדורות over time. Rabbi Harris remembers that the Rosh ישיבה was surprised to hear that 9th graders didn't wear jackets in the streets.[^9])</span>
8. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The blue shirts are an impediment to others not from out ישיבה from being able to accept our ישיבה and gain from the מעלות that we have to offer.[^9]</span>

# The Thank You Hashem Movement
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">While there is some positive to the Thank You Hashem movement, overall, it is mostly negative.[^13]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The Thank You Hashem movement promotes the idea that one should be happy that they are loved by Hashem, and that's it. One doesn't need to grow and work on themselves, learn תורה, or do מצוות. A person simply needs to appreciate the fact that Hashem loves him.
   While it is certainly true that Hashem loves everyone, it does not mean that a person doesn't need to do anything.[^13]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There are certain מעלות, such as the fact that this may cause a person may work on their הכרת הטוב, and it can certainly make people feel happy (at least temporarily until they hit a real ניסיון). For some people, this is important and may be necessary (like Adults at Risk, for example).
   However, it drags in a lot of other people for whom this is not beneficial for.
   (There certainly has to be some truth in the movement because something that has no truth can't catch on. As such, it is clear that there is some truth. However, this is a distortion.)[^13]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In addition, making major changes in יהדות requires real גדולים. In previous generations, we had people like Rav Yisroel Salanter who was able to introduce the מוסר movement (<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">I wasn't sure if rebbi was saying that we don't have such גדולים anymore, or if he was just giving an example of a major change that was instituted by a גדול</span>).
   The Thank You Hashem movement was not started by a person or group of people that have such shoulders that they can institute such changes.[^13]</span>
# Causes of Increased Antisemitism
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The ראש ישיבה felt that in our times, we don't have גדולים who are big enough to know the exact reason for a particular negative event.[^14]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Regardless, one should try to use these events as wakeup calls to try and increase one's עבודת Hashem.[^14]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Many people try to give explanations for why the גויים hate us and give suggestions on how to try and fix it. This is not the correct way to view things. גויים naturally hate us and every so often, for whatever reason, they do not. The only way to "combat" this is by doing the רצון Hashem.[^14]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">That doesn't mean that one can't have positive effects on the way particular גויים behave. As such, one should always try and act like a mensch when dealing with גויים (in addition to the obvious reasons that one should always act like a mensch when dealing with anyone).[^14]
   An interesting illustration of this was with Senator Henry Jackson. He was very in favor of the Jews and so he pushed for a law (called the Jackson-Vanik amendment) which required countries seeking favorable trade status with the US (known as Most Favored Nation status at the time) to allow for free emigration from a country. This law was particularly targeted at the Soviet Union. Part of the reason he pushed for this law was because of his pro-Jew stance. This law was a contributing factor to the eventual mass exodus of Jews in the early 1990s.[^15]
   It is said that the reason why Senator Jackson was so in favor of the Jews was because when he was a child, he grew up in poverty with a single mother who was a cleaning lady for a Jewish family. At some point she got hurt and couldn't work for several weeks. Despite this, the Jewish family kept paying her because they knew she was pressed for money. Because of this חסד this family did, it is possible that well over one million Jews were able to leave Russia.[^16]</span>
# Assumptions that Something is מותר if Everyone Does it
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Question: A person hears that a גדול says that a certain widespread activity is אסור. Is one allowed to assume that there is some basis for the action (since everyone is doing it)?[^17]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If this has been a widespread activity for numerous generations then perhaps one may be able to make such an assumption. However, if one doesn't know of such a tradition, one can't assume such. People these days don't ask questions and just do things because everyone else is doing them.[^17]</span>
# Rolex Raffles
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Some organizations raise money by selling raffle tickets for Rolexes and similar types of items.[^18]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This is not proper. This develops one desire for excessiveness and גשמיות.&lrm;[^18]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">What is considered "excessive" is perhaps difficult to define exactly, but many times (such as a $75k watch) it is quite obvious.[^18]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There is a shmuess about Shevna Hasofer who was a tremendous תלמיד חכם, but he ended up turning into a traitor and threw it all away. The 'גמ says that this happened because the תורה was unable penetrate him because he was a בעל hanaah. The ראש ישיבה points out that the 'גמ does not say that he did עבירות, but, simply, that he was a בעל hanaah. Developing one's תאוה for גשמיות is a repelling force to תורה.&lrm;[^18]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Not only is bad for the person themselves (as it develops one's תאוה for גשמיות), it raises the standards for everyone and it pushes others to try and follow suit, even though many times they can't afford it.[^18]</span>
# Maintaining High Standards in ישיבה
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The ראש ישיבה had a השקפה for the ישיבה to maintain high standards, even beyond the level of the individuals in the ישיבה.
   The reason for this is because a person gains from being part of an institution that is purely focused on תורה and doing the רצון Hashem. The more the institution can reflect such a mission without compromise, the stronger this effect is on one (presumably, within limits. If the ישיבה is so far beyond people that they can't relate to it at all, the perhaps with effect will be lessened).[^19]</span>
