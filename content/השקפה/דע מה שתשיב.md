# Introduction to Learning about אמונה
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is very normal to perhaps not be so interested in אמונה-type topics when one is in ישיבה. However, as one leaves ישיבה, they start interacting with non-Jews and one starts becoming confronted with questions that they never thought about.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is important for oneself to have the answers to these types of questions.[^1]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There is no such thing as the objectively correct answer. The right answer is the one that seems to make sense and feels true to you.
   Obviously, there are objectively wrong answers which are simply the wrong answer no matter how you feel about it.
   What we mean is that sometimes there are multiple answers to the same question, for example, for some questions there may be a more mystical answer and a practical answer. Which answer you relate to better depends on who you are.[^1]</span>
# Responding to Others' Questions
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">As said just above in terms of oneself, the idea that multiple answers can be objectively true but one may relate to one better than another applies when responding to others' questions. The right answer to give depends on who they are.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Similarly, you need to give over an answer that you yourself relate to, as well. If you are giving over a script, it will come across.[^1]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Sometimes questions come from an emotional place (someone suffered a tragedy or abuse, for example). These people are not looking for an intellectual answer and giving them one won't help. Keep in mind that it is fine that you are not able to give these people the answer that they are looking for.[^1]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is important to be able to define things for one's own sake because you can't believe in things that you simply do not understand.[^1]</span>
## Not Having Answers
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">It is okay if you get "stumped" by a question. You don't need to throw out all of your belief. Even if there is a question on what you believe, there are still many reasons for why you believe what you believe. You can circle back to this question when you have a better understanding.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Atheists may have an easier time giving over their understanding of things. That doesn't inherently mean that they are correct, although it may seem so.
   The reason that it is easier for them to explain their side to you is because you understand the axioms that he is working with and so he doesn't need to spell them out.
   You are working with ideas that he doesn't know anything about and so you have to give a long-winded introduction, constructing a whole foundation before you may even get to the point that you are talking about.
   For example, if someone from the ancient past asks you what a computer is and how it works, if you wanted to give anything beyond a basic description, you would have to start explaining things like electricity, electrical circuits, and logic gates, and even at that point you are still nowhere near explaining how the computer works.[^1]</span>
# What Does "G-d" Mean?
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">G-d is defined by the רמח"ל and the רמב"ם.&lrm;[^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In this world, everything that happens is caused by something else. Everything is a result of cause and effect. It is incomprehensible to us for something to happen without a cause.[^2]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">However, if this is true, how does existence make sense? How could anything have come into existence?[^2]</span>
