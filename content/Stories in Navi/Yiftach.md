# Yiftach's Middos
## Pursuer of Peace
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach was willing to forgo what was rightfully his to make peace. Yiftach's brothers chased Yiftach away as they didn't want him to inherit with them. The correct halacha is that Yiftach was allowed to inherit. Yiftach was a mighty warrior and he could have fought them on it. However, he chose to make peace.[^1][^2]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In fact, Yiftach made sure to move to a place outside of Eretz Yisroel so he wouldn't be חייב to be oleh regel or eat maaser sheini in Yerushalayim because he didn't want to come across his brothers or the zikeinim of his city that didn't defend him as this might lead Yiftach to quarrel with them.[^2]</span>
## Humble
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Because he was the son of a zonah, he was humble. This was a quality that drew the zikeinim to him to choose him for a leader, meaning, that because he was the son of a zonah, they knew he would be humble as he came from lowly origins.[^3]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach ascribed his appointment as the leader and his future to the zikeinim appointing him, rather than his own greatness (which was the truth), a further testament to his humility.[^4]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Even after being chosen as the leader, Yiftach did not feel gaavah. This allowed to have bitachon in Hashem and daven for mercy on behalf of Klal Yisroel.[^5]</span>
# Yiftach and his Daughter
## Basic Storyline Without Peirushim
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach asked Hashem for help in fighting against Amon and Yiftach promised that he would bring the first thing that would come from his house as a karban olah if he was victorious.[^6]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach was victorious, but the first thing that stepped out of Yiftach's house was his daughter, who was coming to congratulate her father and celebrate the victory.[^7]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach was distraught by the fact that his daughter was the first thing to leave his house, but he felt he had no other choice. His daughter agreed with him, but she asked for two months to go mourn with her friends about the fact that she would never be able to get married, which Yiftach granted.[^8]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At the end of the two months, Yiftach fulfilled his promise.[^9]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Every year after that, for 4 days a year, Klal Yisroel mourned the loss of Yiftach's daughter in such a tragic fashion.[^10]</span>
## Storyline as Discussed by the Mefarshim
### Yiftach's Promise
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The lashon of the passuk by Yiftach's promise is "וְהָיָה֙ לַה' וְהַעֲלִיתִ֖יהוּ עֹלָֽה". Some peirushim note this double lashon ("'וְהָיָה֙ לַה" and "וְהַעֲלִיתִ֖יהוּ עֹלָֽה") and learn from here that Yiftach made a condition in his promise. He promised to give the first thing that comes out of his house to Hashem, meaning, that it will be kodesh, but not that it will be brought up as a karban, *or* he will bring the first thing as a karban. The deciding factor for which he was to choose (meaning, would it be kodesh, or would it be brought as a karban) was whether the thing that left his house first was fitting to be brought as a karban or not.[^11][^12][^13][^14]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There are other examples when a "ו" may mean "or" instead of "and". As such, the "ו" in "וְהַעֲלִיתִ֖יהוּ" should be read as an "or".[^11]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach's daughter asks to mourn with her friends about the fact that she would never get married, not about the fact that she was going to die. It sounds like she did not die, but instead, had to remain unmarried.[^11]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The passuk cryptically says that Yiftach fulfilled his promise. It does not say that Yiftach brought his daughter up as a karban.[^11]</span>
