# Basic Storyline Without Peirushim
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach asked Hashem for help in fighting against Amon and Yiftach promised that he would bring the first thing that would come from his house as a karban olah if he was victorious.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach was victorious, but the first thing that stepped out of Yiftach's house was his daughter, who was coming to congratulate her father and celebrate the victory.[^2]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach was distraught by the fact that his daughter was the first thing to leave his house, but he felt he had no other choice. His daughter agreed with him, but she asked for two months to go mourn with her friends about the fact that she would never be able to get married, which Yiftach granted.[^3]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At the end of the two months, Yiftach fulfilled his promise.[^4]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Every year after that, for 4 days a year, כלל ישראל mourned the loss of Yiftach's daughter in such a tragic fashion.[^5]</span>
# Storyline as Discussed by the מפרשים
## What Happened to Yiftach's Daughter
### She Was Sacrificed as a Karban
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">She was actually sacrificed as a karban.[^6][^6][^7][^8] This was obviously incorrect[^6][^6][^8] and Yiftach was punished for this ([[#Yiftach's and Pinchas's Punishments|see later]]).</span>

### She Lived Alone Focusing on Dveikus with Hashem
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach built a house for her to live in, alone, and focus on hisbodidus and dveikus with Hashem. She never got married.[^9][^10]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">For 4 days a year, people were allowed to visit her.[^10]</span>
#### Why She Was not Permitted to Get Married
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to some, Yiftach's daughter was not permitted to get married because a women has a responsibility to her husband. The promise was that Yiftach's daughter would solely be focused on Hashem, not on anyone else, so she could not get married.[^11]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Others say that Yiftach thought that this was a holier state of being.[^12]</span>
#### Indications From the Navi
##### Yiftach's Promise
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The lashon of the passuk by Yiftach's promise is "וְהָיָה֙ לַה' וְהַעֲלִיתִ֖יהוּ עֹלָֽה". Some peirushim note this double lashon ("'וְהָיָה֙ לַה" and "וְהַעֲלִיתִ֖יהוּ עֹלָֽה") and learn from here that Yiftach made a condition in his promise. He promised to give the first thing that comes out of his house to Hashem, meaning, that it will be kodesh, but not that it will be brought up as a karban, *or* he will bring the first thing as a karban. The deciding factor for which he was to choose (meaning, would it be kodesh, or would it be brought as a karban) was whether the thing that left his house first was fitting to be brought as a karban or not.[^13][^14][^15][^16]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">There are other examples when a "ו" may mean "or" instead of "and". As such, the "ו" at the beginning of "וְהַעֲלִיתִ֖יהוּ" should be read as an "or".[^13]</span>
##### Mourning with Friends
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach's daughter asks to mourn with her friends about the fact that she would never get married, not about the fact that she was going to die. It sounds like she did not die, but instead, had to remain unmarried.[^13]</span>
##### Yiftach "Fulfilled His Promise"
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The passuk cryptically says that Yiftach fulfilled his promise. It does not say that Yiftach brought his daughter up as a karban.[^13]</span>
	1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;"><span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">Counter:</span> The navi was relying on what was previously mentioned, namely, that he promised to offer as a karban the first thing that leaves his home. In addition, the passuk may have not wanted to explicitly say that Yiftach killed his daughter because it was a disgraceful thing.[^8]</span>
## Hataras Nedarim and the Validity of the Promise
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">חז"ל say that Yiftach's daughter went to the Sanhedrin and asked them to be meifir the neder. However, they were unable to do it.[^17]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">חז"ל say that Pinchas could have been matir the neder. However, Yiftach felt that as the leader of the כלל ישראל, Pinchas should come to him. Meanwhile, Pinchas felt that since Yiftach needed him, Yiftach should come. Between the two of them the neder was not matired and Yiftach's daughter had to suffer the consequences. Both Pinchas and Yiftach were punished.[^18]</span> ^ccc626
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In addition, Yiftach was never chayiv to bring her as an olah at all. There is a machlokes about what happens if one designates a tamei animal or a baal mum as an olah. It is a machlokes if the neder doesn't count at all, or that the person needs to take money equivalent to the value of the animal and use that to buy an animal to be brought as an olah. The same applied here to Yiftach's daughter.[^19]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach and his daughter didn't know this and so he acted on the neder.[^20]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to this, when it says that Pinchas could have been matir the neder, it may mean that he could have told Yiftach that all he needed to do was give the monetary value of his daughter to buy a karban[^20][^21] or it may mean that even though there was no neder at all, one still needs to have a תלמיד chacham tell him this, as we don't want people to take nedarim lightly.[^21]</span>
6. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Also according to this, Yiftach thought that he needed to literally complete his neder as he said and he couldn't just give money, and so he would need legitimate hataras nedarim to be able to get out of it. Even when the Sanhedrin told Yiftach the דין that all he had to do was give money, Yiftach didn't believe them.[^22]</span>
7. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to some, regardless of the requirement to satisfy the neder, Yiftach and those who lived around him didn't know that hataras nedarim could be done. Either they didn't know about hataras nedarim at all because the תורה had been abandoned for too long, or that Yiftach thought that because this promise was made in recognition of Hashem's neis, he thought that it would be improper to have charatah.[^23] (While not explicitly said, it would seem that both of these points were incorrect.)</span>

## Yiftach's and Pinchas's Punishments
### For Making a Risky Neder
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Because Yiftach made a neder which was extremely open to chance (a בהמה טמאה or a person could have come out first), Hashem punished Yiftach by causing his daughter to be the thing that came out of his house first.[^24]</span>
	1. This may only be according to those that say that Yiftach killed his daughter as many who said that he didn't kill her hold that Yiftach made a condition in his neder depending on what came out first. As such, I don't think he did anything wrong according to those opinions.
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The punishment wasn't that Yiftach was supposed to act on his neder and hurt his daughter, as why would it make sense for Yiftach's daughter to suffer for her father's עבירה? The punishment was supposed to be that Yiftach wouldn't be able to fulfill his neder in the ideal fashion, and that he would have to give money or have to travel to a תלמיד chacham and tell them what he did to be matir his neder. Obviously, that is not what ended up happening.[^25]</span>
### For Acting on the Neder
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach's punishment was that he was stuck with a disease that made his limbs slowly fall off. This is hinted to by the passuk that says that "Yiftach was buried in the cit*ies* of Gilad"[^26] (it sounds like he was buried in multiple cities). חז"ל say that wherever his limb would fall off, it would be buried there.[^18]</span> ^6b4328
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Pinchas was punished with losing his connection to the shechina. This is hinted to by the pasuk where Pinchas says "Hashem used to be with me"[^27] (implying that Hashem was no longer with him).[^18] (See [[#^ccc626|here]] for why Pinchas was punished, as well.)</span>
