# Civil War with Epraim
## Basic Storyline With Minimal Peirush
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Shevet Ephraim angry at Yiftach and so they confronted him.[^1]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Ephraim was so incensed that they threatened to burn down Yiftach's house.[^2]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach responded that he had asked them in the past and they had not come. In addition, he had been victorious without them.[^3]</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Fighting broke out between Yiftach and his men against Ephraim.[^4]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach's men guarded all of the bridges over the Yarden and whenever someone would come by they would compel the person to say a word that people from Ephraim would not be able to pronounce properly (then would pronounce an "sh" sound like an "s").[^5]</span>
6. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If the person was proven to be from shevet Ephraim, he would be killed.[^6]</span>
7. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">In all 42,000 from shevet Ephraim were killed.[^6]</span>
## Storyline as Discussed by the Mefarshim
### What was Ephraim Angry About
#### Yiftach Fought Without Them
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Gideon, a previous shofet, had also fought against the enemies of Klal Yisroel without Ephraim, and they were insulted the, as well.[^7] The fact that now it happened again compounded the insult.[^8]</span>
#### Yiftach was Chosen as Leader Without Their Imput
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Ephraim was upset because they felt that Gilad was a relatively unimportant part of shevet Yosef.[^9][^10][^11] This was because they were either entirely across the Yarden (which only had half of Menashe, as opposed to the Eretz Yisroel side which had the other half of Menashe plus the entire Ephraim)[^11] or they were actually divided: half of Gilad was across the Yarden in shevet Menashe's land, and half were in Eretz Yisroel, in shevet Ephraim's land.[^10] As such, they did not feel that Gilad had the right to choose the leader.[^10][^12]</span> ^a260b0
### How Ephraim Insulted Yiftach
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The simple reading of the passuk is that Ephraim threatened to burn down Yiftach's house with him inside.[^2] (The מצודת דוד learns the passuk this way.[^13])</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to the מנחה קטנה, Ephraim only threatened to destroy Yiftach's house, but not with him inside. Yiftach's house (which was presumably nice as he was the shofet), was an honor for him. By burning down Yiftach's house, and attacking his honor through the destruction of something physical, Ephraim hoped Yiftach would learn that all the more so, he should care about the honor of humans (meaning, the honor of Ephraim).[^14]</span>
3. Additionally, Ephraim called Gilad an unimportant part of shevet Yosef, [[#^a260b0|as discussed]].
### Could Ephraim Have Been Appeased
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to some mefarshim, Ephraim came in with the intent to fight and were not going to accept an apology. This was because Gideon had similarly not included them, and then apologized afterwards. The fact that it happened again showed Ephraim that the apology was insincere and they were not going to be willing to accept one now.[^14]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The מלבי"ם says that Yiftach responded gently but Ephraim didn't listen.[^11]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to other mefarshim, Yiftach could have and should have attempted to appease Ephraim.[^15] There mefarshim would seem to hold that Yiftach's response was not so apologetic.</span>
### Yiftach's Response
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Amon largely (perhaps entirely) focused in the people of Gilad (Yiftach's extended family).[^16][^17]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach had asked them for assistance in other battles and they had not come[^18][^17] in which case the hypocrisy that they hadn't come to fight against Amon and are only coming now to fight Yiftach is glaringly aparent.[^19]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach had asked for their assistance in this battle, as well, but Hashem prevented them from coming because Hashem didn't want them to take part in the victory. It was clear from this, and the fact that Yiftach had won in miraculous fashion (he had won with a small army) Yiftach added that clearly Hashem would not be on their side if they fought against him.[^16]</span>
	1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to this, I don't even begin to understand what Ephraim was upset about. What did they mean that Yiftach didn't invite them if Yiftach, in fact, did? Perhaps the message was lost on the way, or only arrived there after the battle was over?</span>
4. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Ephraim had ample opportunity to save Klal Yisroel as Amon had been antagonizing Klal Yisroel for years. If they were ever going to do it, they had their chance.[^20]</span>
5. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach told Ephraim that he had been kicked out from his family and that had turned him into a humble person. As such, he did not leave Ephraim out because he thought he was so great he didn't need them or anything like that.[^21]</span>
6. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach pointed out that he had risked his life, and he had won through only with a נס<span style="visibility:hidden">l</span>[^22][^20] (he only had a small army against Amon[^22]).</span>
7. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach told Ephraim that if he had lost, Ephraim would have a reasonable: perhaps if he would have included Ephraim in his army he would have won. But now that he was victorious without Ephraim, their complaint was unjustified.[^23]</span>
### Who From Ephraim Fought
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">According to numerous mefarshim, the entire shevet Ephraim was involved in this fight.[^24][^14]</span>
	1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">I am confused by the מנחה קטנה who says that the whole shevet fought, but then over [[#^51c48b|here]] implies that it was only some of the shevet. Perhaps he means that a very high percentage fought, and the מנחה קטנה just calls it the whole shevet, even though it wasn't quite everyone?</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">The רד"ק says that it was the "רקים והפחותים" of Ephraim.[^25]</span>
### Ascertaining Who was Guilty
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At the river crossings that Yiftach's men were guarding, they would ask the travelers what shevet they were from. If they would answer that they were from Ephraim, then Yiftach's men would look into the matter somewhat, and ultimately let them go. If they had nothing to hide then that would show that they were not guilty.[^26]</span> ^51c48b
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">If they would say that they were from some from a shevet other than Ephraim and then they would not be able to make the "sh" sound, that would show that they lied, which would indicate their guilt.[^26]</span>
### Responsibility for the Tragedy
#### Ephraim's Responsibility

1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This was a punishment for aveiros that Ephraim had done in secrecy.[^25]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Ephraim should have been grateful to Yiftach for having gotten rid of the enemies of Klal Yisroel and risked his life to do so. Not only were they not grateful, but they were even antagonistic.[^15]</span>
3. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">This was a punishment for Avimelech, an evil shofet of Klal Yisroel, who's followers and base of operations were in Shichem. This was a city in shevet Ephraim's territory, and they should have protested Avimelech's actions. They were punished for not doing so.[^14]</span>
#### Yiftach's Responsibility
1. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">At the same time, Yiftach should have tried appeasing them as Gideon had done earlier in a similar circumstance.[^27][^15]</span>
2. <span style="display: block; text-align: justify;">Yiftach died in a horrible way (see [[#^6b4328|here]]), but the medrash (and the mefarshim presenting the medrash) say that that was a punishment for not being matir neder on his daughter. Almost nobody says that it was at least partically a punishment for killing Ephraim.[^28]</span>
